\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Projective spectral filters such as the rodeo algorithm
prepare eigenstates with an accuracy that improves exponentially in the
number of filter cycles, at a cost set by the squared overlap $p$ of the
input state with the target.  Resolution refinement, the adiabatic
transport of an eigenstate from a coarse to a fine single-particle basis
along a shift-controlled, gapped path, has been proposed to supply that
input without variational optimization.
\textbf{Purpose:} To implement the complete chain --- coarse eigenstate,
prolongation, resolution refinement, rodeo filter --- at the gate level on
the exactly solvable constant-pairing Hamiltonian, to validate every stage
against exact results, and to establish on a common observable what the
refinement stage contributes relative to the simplest alternative inputs to
the same filter.
\textbf{Methods:} All stages are compiled to single-qubit rotations and
controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates, with second-order product formulas for the
refinement and for the controlled evolution of the filter, and are simulated
exactly within the particle-number sector.  The benchmarks are full
configuration interaction, coupled-cluster doubles, unitary coupled-cluster
singles and doubles (UCCSD) at the operator and at the gate level, and the
analytic success-probability formulas of the filter.  The comparison uses
the ground-state infidelity as the common observable and the two-qubit gate
count per accepted preparation.
\textbf{Results:} For four particles on four levels (eight system qubits)
the refinement raises the overlap of the prolonged determinant from $0.87$
to $0.998$ along a path that stays gapped, with no trained parameter and no
measurement during preparation, and the filter removes the remaining weight
geometrically, in agreement with the exact Gaussian-ensemble expression
derived here, with an acceptance that converges to $p$ and an accuracy floor
of $1.2\times10^{-4}$ set by the product formula; the acceptance statistics
of the filter diagnose a corrupted evolution.  The response norm of the path
is obtained in closed form at its start, and the available sufficient
adiabatic-time and Trotter-step conditions overestimate the empirical cost
by two to three orders of magnitude.  At matched accuracy, an ordinary adiabatic ramp from the
Hartree--Fock Hamiltonian reaches the same overlap at one quarter of the
refinement cost, and a four-parameter pair-rotation ansatz of $192$ CNOT
gates reaches an infidelity of $1.8\times10^{-4}$ without any filter.  For
four pairs on eight levels (sixteen qubits) the ansatz degrades to
$3\times10^{-3}$ and filter cycles become eleven times dearer; refinement
followed by the filter is then $1.5$--$2$ times cheaper than the
variational route at every target, and within $4$--$19\%$ of the plain
ramp once the shift is scaled with the size.
\textbf{Conclusions:} Resolution refinement followed by the rodeo algorithm
is a validated, optimizer-free and measurement-free route to a
high-fidelity correlated eigenstate, with every gate counted and every stage
anchored to exact results.  At eight qubits it is not the cheapest route; at
sixteen it overtakes the variational preparation but not the ordinary
adiabatic ramp, so its specific advantage must come from a coarse eigenstate
better than the determinant, which this hierarchy does not exercise.  A companion paper extends the assessment to a seniority-breaking
particle-hole interaction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ===========================================================================
Determining the low-lying eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a many-body Hamiltonian is
one of the grand challenges of quantum many-body theory, where the Hilbert-space
dimension grows exponentially with the number of constituents.  Quantum computers
offer new routes to eigenvalue estimation and eigenstate preparation, and their
prospects for nuclear structure have been mapped in recent community
studies~\cite{Beck2023,Bauer2023,LeeReview2023}, with schematic nuclear models ---
pairing, Lipkin, and shell-model Hamiltonians --- serving as the standard proving
ground~\cite{Ovrum2007,Hlatshwayo2022,Stetcu2022,PerezObiol2023,Jiang2023,Zhang2024,Lacroix2023}.
The state-of-the-art preparation methods, however, each face an obstacle at scale.
The variational quantum eigensolver (VQE)~\cite{Peruzzo2014} is limited by the cost
of its classical optimization loop, by the accuracy floor of its ansatz and, for
sufficiently expressive circuits, by barren plateaus --- gradients that vanish
exponentially with system size~\cite{McClean2018,Larocca2025}.  Adiabatic state
preparation requires evolution times that diverge if the gap between the tracked
state and the rest of the spectrum closes along the path~\cite{Albash2018}.
Projective spectral filters --- quantum phase estimation~\cite{Abrams1999},
polynomial filters based on quantum signal processing~\cite{LinTong2020,Dong2022},
projection by measurement~\cite{Stetcu2023}, and the rodeo algorithm
(RA)~\cite{Choi2021,Qian2024,Cohen2023,BeeLindgren2022,Patkowski2026} --- converge
exponentially in the number of cycles, but only once the initial state has a
significant squared overlap $p$ with the target eigenstate; their cost grows as
$1/p$, and for a fixed, unoptimized reference state $p$ falls exponentially with
system size.

These limitations are complementary, and the idea of \emph{preconditioning} the
input of a projective filter by an adiabatic stage is as old as the rodeo
algorithm itself: the original proposal~\cite{Choi2021} already combines a
short adiabatic ramp with the filter, the hierarchical fusion method of
Ref.~\cite{Patkowski2025fusion} builds large systems from exactly solvable
subsystems by alternating adiabatic ramps and rodeo cycles, and the
resolution-refinement proposal~\cite{Bogner2026} explicitly recommends
switching from adiabatic evolution to a filter such as the rodeo algorithm once
the overlap is large.  \emph{Resolution refinement} bootstraps an eigenstate
across model-space scales: an eigenstate of a coarse (low-resolution)
Hamiltonian is lifted by a prolongation operator into a finer space and
adiabatically evolved to the fine-resolution eigenstate.  Because refinement
changes the low-energy spectrum only mildly, the interpolation path can be
kept gapped, and under the additional assumption that the first-order
perturbative correction to the tracked state stays bounded~\cite{Bogner2026}
the required adiabatic time scales as the inverse gap times a slowly growing
system-size factor rather than as the inverse gap squared.  The same
bootstrapping idea applied along the other axis is \emph{nucleation}, proposed
recently by some of us~\cite{Sadia2026}: the exact ground state of a small
lattice (the seed) is compiled into a circuit, and the lattice is then grown
in stages, each stage a Trotterized adiabatic interpolation that couples newly
added qubits to the existing bulk, until the target size is reached.
Refinement keeps the domain fixed and raises the resolution; nucleation keeps
the resolution fixed and grows the domain.  Both replace one hard global
preparation by a sequence of local, gapped adiabatic steps that start from an
exactly solved problem, both are compiled as Trotterized adiabatic circuits,
and Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} supplies for such circuits sufficient conditions on
the evolution time and on the number of Trotter steps, together with a
variational variant that reuses the Trotterized circuit as a warm-started
ansatz.  The present work
implements this chain --- (i) prepare a low-resolution eigenstate; (ii) prolong
and adiabatically refine for a time $T$; (iii) apply the rodeo algorithm ---
for a hierarchy of fermionic single-particle bases, and asks the question that
decides whether the refinement stage earns its gates: does refinement followed
by filtering beat filtering the best input that is available cheaply?

The pairing problem has become a standard target for quantum algorithms, and
the program of Lacroix and collaborators is the closest in spirit to ours.
Its common thread is the deliberate use of symmetry breaking and
restoration: a symmetry-breaking (BCS-like) trial state is cheap and
correlated, and the symmetry is restored afterwards by a projective step ---
quantum phase estimation used as a projector~\cite{Lacroix2020}, Grover-type
oracles~\cite{RuizGuzman2023}, spin filters~\cite{Siwach2021} or classical
shadows~\cite{RuizGuzman2024} --- with the restored state then handed to a
spectral post-processing stage such as phase estimation or a Krylov
expansion~\cite{RuizGuzman2022,RuizGuzman2021}; Refs.~\cite{Lacroix2023EPJA,Lacroix2023}
review this viewpoint and Ref.~\cite{Ayral2023} the broader many-body landscape.  More
recently the same group has used ADAPT-VQE on pairing Hamiltonians with
symmetry-conserving and symmetry-breaking operator pools~\cite{Zhang2024},
extracted excited states from the subspace spanned along the ADAPT convergence
path~\cite{Zhang2025}, obtained Lipkin spectra with a quantum
equation-of-motion approach~\cite{Hlatshwayo2022} and with a two-qubit
generator-coordinate scheme~\cite{BeaujeaultTaudiere2024}, and
built one-body Green's functions of small superfluid systems from a
variational even-$N$ ground state and a quantum subspace expansion for its
$N\pm1$ neighbors~\cite{AychetClaisse2026}.  Our pipeline addresses the step
that all of these share --- obtaining a high-fidelity eigenstate of the
fine-resolution Hamiltonian from a cheap approximate one --- with a different
mechanism: not symmetry projection or subspace diagonalization, but an
adiabatic path across resolutions followed by an energy filter; the relation
is taken up in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}.

In this work we realize the full pipeline end to end on a transparent and exactly
solvable testbed, the constant-pairing Hamiltonian familiar from nuclear and
condensed-matter many-body theory~\cite{LNP936} and integrable by the Richardson
ansatz~\cite{Richardson1963,RichardsonSherman1964,Dukelsky2004}.  Its exact
solvability is precisely what makes it the right laboratory: every stage of the
pipeline can be validated quantitatively --- the gate-level UCCSD coarse solver
against full configuration interaction (FCI) and coupled-cluster doubles (CCD); the
prolongation and adiabatic refinement against the exact high-resolution eigenstate;
and the rodeo filter against the analytic success-probability formulas of
Ref.~\cite{Qian2024}.  All circuits are implemented explicitly as rotation and CNOT
gates (and an ancilla-based rodeo cycle), so the demonstration doubles as a
reference implementation for hardware.

Relative to the resolution-refinement proposal of Ref.~\cite{Bogner2026}, the
rodeo literature~\cite{Choi2021,Qian2024} and the fusion
method~\cite{Patkowski2025fusion}, which established adiabatic preconditioning
of the filter on lattice and spin systems, the present work contributes five
elements: (i) an implementation of refinement for a hierarchy of
\emph{single-particle bases} in a fermionic pairing problem, where the
prolongation is gate-free and the embedded coarse Hamiltonian is a conditioned
operator whose circuit we give and count, at eight and at sixteen qubits; (ii) a quantitative benchmark of the
refinement-plus-filter chain against the same filter applied to the unrefined
input, to an ordinary adiabatic ramp, to a prolonged coarse variational state
and to full-space variational states --- the UCCSD template and the
four-parameter pair-rotation ansatz that reproduces it exactly --- with the
ground-state infidelity as the common observable, matched evolution accuracy,
and the minimum cost at a given target accuracy, filter cycles included or not; (iii) a gate-level
compilation of every stage --- Trotterized refinement and Trotterized
controlled evolution included --- with a two-qubit-gate budget per accepted
preparation that separates one-time training, repeated preparation and the
filter; (iv) an evaluation of the rigorous sufficient conditions on the
adiabatic time and on the Trotter step count of Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} for the
refinement path, against the resources actually needed; and (v) a reproducible
reference implementation in which every figure and number derives from a
single executable notebook.  The outcome of (ii) is stated plainly: at the
system sizes accessible to exact validation here, refinement followed by
filtering does not outperform filtering a variational input, is roughly
tied with filtering the unrefined prolonged state at matched fidelity, and
costs four times more than an ordinary adiabatic ramp from the Hartree--Fock
Hamiltonian that reaches the same overlap.  Doubling the space to four
pairs on eight levels (sixteen qubits, Sec.~\ref{sec:large}) reverses the
first of these verdicts --- the variational input degrades and the filter
cycles become expensive, so refinement plus filter is then $1.5$--$2$
times cheaper than the variational route --- but not the last: the plain
ramp from the same determinant stays cheaper by the cost of the embedded
coarse term.  The case for the refinement stage rests on its freedom from
classical optimization and training measurements, and on coarse eigenstates
that are better than the determinant, which this hierarchy does not
exercise.

The pairing force conserves seniority, and that symmetry is what keeps the
model transparent: the ground state lives in the seniority-zero subspace, the
one-particle--one-hole channel is closed, and single excitations drop out of
the variational ansatz.  We keep to this seniority-conserving model
throughout the present paper, as does most of the quantum-algorithm
literature on pairing cited above.  In a companion paper~\cite{PHcompanion}
we remove the simplification by adding a seniority-breaking particle-hole
interaction and repeat the complete assessment --- exact and variational
references, refinement, filtering and the circuit-level budget --- on the
resulting Hamiltonian, which carries the one-particle--one-hole channel of
realistic two-body interactions.

\section{The pairing model and its qubit encoding}
% ===========================================================================
We study the constant-pairing Hamiltonian
\begin{widetext}
\begin{equation}
  H=\delta\sum_{p=0}^{k-1} p\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow\downarrow} n_{p\sigma}
   -\frac{g}{2}\sum_{p,q=0}^{k-1} a^\dagger_{p\uparrow}a^\dagger_{p\downarrow}
     a_{q\downarrow}a_{q\uparrow},
  \label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
\end{widetext}
with $k$ doubly degenerate single-particle levels of spacing $\delta$ (set to
$\delta=1$) and a pairing strength $g$.  The interaction scatters time-reversed
pairs between levels; it conserves the seniority (the number of unpaired particles),
so the ground state of $N$ paired particles lives entirely in the seniority-zero
subspace, and the model is exactly solvable by the Richardson
ansatz~\cite{Richardson1963,RichardsonSherman1964,Dukelsky2004} --- the property
that anchors every benchmark below.  We map level $p$ and spin $\sigma$ to qubit $2p+\sigma$ (so a $k$-level
problem uses $2k$ qubits) and encode the fermionic operators through the
Jordan--Wigner transformation.  The Hartree--Fock (HF) reference fills the lowest
$N/2$ levels, with $E_{\rm HF}(N{=}4,g)=2-g$.

Throughout we use $N=4$ particles as the running example.  The fixed-$N$ sector
dimension is $\binom{2k}{N}$, growing from $1$ at $k=2$ (a single filled
determinant) to $70$ at $k=4$ and $1820$ at $k=8$; the seniority-zero ground-state
problem is correspondingly compact, which makes the model an ideal multi-resolution
laboratory.  ``Basis refinement'' here means \emph{increasing $k$ at fixed $N$}: the
number of single-particle levels (and qubits) grows while the particle number is
held fixed.

\section{The hybrid pipeline}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Coarse-state preparation: gate-level UCCSD--VQE}
\label{sec:uccsd}
The coarse eigenstate is prepared with a unitary coupled-cluster ansatz restricted
to singles and doubles (UCCSD)~\cite{LeeUCC2019,Tilly2022}, Trotterized into a
product of excitation circuits acting on the HF reference,
\begin{equation}
  \ket{\Psi(\boldsymbol\theta)}
  =\prod_{\mu} e^{\theta_\mu(\hat\tau_\mu-\hat\tau_\mu^\dagger)}\ket{\rm HF}.
\end{equation}
Through the Jordan--Wigner map each anti-Hermitian generator becomes a sum of
commuting Pauli strings, so every factor compiles into a basis change, a CNOT ladder,
a single $R_z(\theta)$ rotation, and the uncompute; a single excitation yields two
Pauli-rotation sub-circuits and a double excitation yields eight (the circuits are
shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:circ-vqe}).  The ordered product differs in general from
the exponential of the summed cluster operator, so the ansatz is the
\emph{factorized} (disentangled) UCCSD, and the operator order --- singles
first, then doubles, each in the lexicographic order of the pool --- is part
of its definition.  We optimize with the exact analytic gradient of the
product ansatz: a fermionic excitation generator has eigenvalues $0$ and
$\pm i$, so the dependence of the energy on one parameter contains first
\emph{and} second harmonics, and the single-frequency two-term parameter-shift
rule is not sufficient in general.  Because the pairing force does not couple
to seniority-changing single excitations, their energy gradient vanishes
identically on the seniority-zero manifold; in the zero-initialized
optimizations used here all singles amplitudes stay at zero to machine
precision and the solver is effectively a \emph{doubles} theory.  (This is a
property of the optimization on the symmetry-preserving manifold, not a
theorem about every unconstrained optimum of an ordered ansatz; and unitary
doubles and classical CCD remain distinct approximations, as the different
energies of Table~\ref{tab:uccsd} show.)  A variational solver supplies an
\emph{approximation} to the coarse ground state,
$\ket{\psi_{\rm low}}=c_0\ket{E^{\rm low}_0}+\sum_{j>0}c_j\ket{E^{\rm low}_j}$
with coarse infidelity $\epsilon_{\rm low}=1-\abs{c_0}^2$; the later stages
are written for an exact coarse eigenstate, and Sec.~\ref{sec:refine} states
what an imperfect input costs.

\subsection{The prolongation operator}
The prolongation $P$ embeds a low-resolution state into the high-resolution space.
For basis refinement it maps every many-body basis state of the $k_{\rm low}$ space to
the \emph{same} occupation pattern in the $k_{\rm high}$ space, leaving the new
single-particle levels empty.  In canonical Jordan--Wigner ordering this is
gate-free; for lattice refinement the analogous operator distributes each occupied
coarse site over the neighboring fine sites with two single-qubit rotations and two
CNOTs per site per spatial direction, i.e.\ $O(N_{\rm sites})$ gates per
dimension~\cite{Bogner2026}.  The prolonged state $\ket{\Phi_0}=P\ket{\psi_{\rm low}}$
satisfies $\expval{\Hhigh}{\Phi_0}=\expval{\Hlow}{\psi_{\rm low}}$ exactly
\emph{because} the nested bare pairing Hamiltonians used here obey
$P^\dagger\Hhigh P=\Hlow$ (same level spacing and coupling at both
resolutions); the identity does not follow from the definition of a
prolongation and can fail for a renormalized or re-parametrized coarse
Hamiltonian.  For the exact coarse eigenstate the right-hand side is
$E_{\rm low}$, for a variational one its variational energy.  That the
prolonged state also has a \emph{large overlap} with the high-resolution
ground state is a separate, empirical property of this pair of Hamiltonians,
documented by the overlaps reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:res-refine}; preservation
of the coarse expectation value does not imply it.

\subsection{Resolution refinement (adiabatic phase)}
\label{sec:refine}
The prolonged state is refined by adiabatically following the interpolated,
energy-shifted Hamiltonian
\begin{widetext}
\begin{equation}
  H(s)=\cos^2\!\Big(\tfrac{\pi s}{2}\Big)\,P(\Hlow-\mu)P^\dagger
      +\sin^2\!\Big(\tfrac{\pi s}{2}\Big)\,(\Hhigh-\mu),
  \qquad s=\frac{t}{T},
  \label{eq:path}
\end{equation}
\end{widetext}
evolved by a Suzuki--Trotter product over total time $T$.  The shift $\mu$
(here $\mu=E_{\rm low}+0.6$) places the tracked low-energy states below the large
null space of $P^\dagger$ at $s=0$; provided the tracked branch stays
isolated from the rest of the spectrum along the whole path --- a condition
that the shift alone does not guarantee and that the spectral calculations
of Secs.~\ref{sec:res-refine}--\ref{sec:res-gscan} verify case by case --- the level followed from
$s=0$ to $s=1$ is the high-resolution ground state.  The shift is not a
global energy offset: writing $\mu PP^\dagger\cos^2(\pi s/2)=\mu\,\mathbb1
\cos^2(\pi s/2)-\mu\cos^2(\pi s/2)(\mathbb1-PP^\dagger)$ exhibits it as a
relative penalty on the complement of the prolonged subspace, which is why
changing $\mu$ changes the path dynamics while leaving the end-point
spectrum untouched.  The first term is \emph{not} the coarse
Hamiltonian acting on the coarse register alone: since $PP^\dagger$ projects on
the states in which the appended single-particle levels are empty,
\begin{equation}
  A\equiv P(\Hlow-\mu)P^\dagger=(\Hlow-\mu)\otimes
  \ket{0\!\cdots\!0}\!\bra{0\!\cdots\!0}_{\rm app},
  \label{eq:Aterm}
\end{equation}
a coarse-space operator conditioned on the $2(k_{\rm high}-k_{\rm low})$ new
qubits being in $\ket{0}$; its circuit and cost are given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized}.  At $s=0$ the gap above the tracked state is
$\min(E_1^{\rm low}-E_0^{\rm low},\,\mu-E_0^{\rm low})$: increasing the shift
lowers the embedded coarse states further below the null space, and the gain
in initial gap saturates at the coarse excitation gap (for a one-dimensional
coarse space, as in the $k=2$ start used below, it is simply $\mu-E_{\rm low}$).
Along the path the instantaneous spectrum is found to stay smooth and gapped
in all cases studied here.  A gapped path alone does not fix the adiabatic
time scale; Ref.~\cite{Bogner2026} shows that when the squared norm of the
first-order perturbative correction to the tracked state,
$\chi(s)=\sum_{n>0}\abs{\mel{n}{\partial_sH}{0}}^2/(E_n-E_0)^2$, stays
uniformly bounded along the path, the adiabatic time scales as
$T\sim1/\Delta E$ rather than as the $O(1/\Delta E_{\min}^2)$ of the generic
adiabatic theorem~\cite{Albash2018}.  Section~\ref{sec:res-gscan} evaluates
$\chi$ for our path family and treats the overlap
$p=\abs{\braket{\Psi_{\rm high}}{\Phi(T)}}^2$ as the figure of merit of the
stage.  Refinement is unitary and does not purify an imperfect coarse
input: if the coarse state carries infidelity $\epsilon_{\rm low}$, ideal
adiabatic continuation transports that weight to excited fine states, and the
final infidelity of the digitized stage obeys
\begin{equation}
  1-F_{\rm refine}\le\big(\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm low}}+a_{\rm ad}+\eta\big)^2,
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
where $a_{\rm ad}$ bounds the leakage amplitude of the ideal evolution started
from the exact coarse ground state and $\eta$ the operator-norm error of the
product formula (decompose the input into its ground and orthogonal
components and apply the triangle inequality to the final component
orthogonal to the target).  The three sources must share a target
$\epsilon$; assigning all of it to the adiabatic term and then adding the
digitization error does not certify the same total.  The residual excited
weight is what the rodeo stage removes, provided the overlap with the target
remains nonzero.

The interpolation~\eqref{eq:path} is the $\sin^2(\pi s/2)$ schedule, whose
vanishing derivatives at both ends remove the boundary terms of the adiabatic
bound of Jansen, Ruskai and Seiler~\cite{Jansen2007}; it is also the schedule
of the nucleation method~\cite{Sadia2026}, where each growth stage couples
newly added qubits to a bulk lattice in the way the second term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:path} couples the appended levels to the coarse space.
Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} turns that bound into explicit sufficient conditions
for a Trotterized adiabatic stage: a total time
\begin{equation}
  T\;\ge\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\int_0^1
  \left[\frac{\norm{\ddot H}}{\Delta^2}+7\,\frac{\norm{\dot H}^2}{\Delta^3}\right]ds
  \label{eq:Tbound}
\end{equation}
guarantees a final infidelity at most $\epsilon$, with derivatives taken with
respect to $s$ and $\Delta(s)$ the instantaneous gap, and
$n_s\ge\eta^{-1}(T\Lambda_1/4+T^2\Lambda_2/2)$ first-order Trotter steps keep
the operator error of the digitized evolution below $\eta$, with
$\Lambda_1=\max_s\norm{\dot H}$ and, for the many groups of our compilation,
$\Lambda_2=\max_s\sum_{i<j}\norm{[H_i(s),H_j(s)]}$, the sum of the
commutator norms over all pairs of groups --- the natural extension of the
two-group statement of Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026}, which is what
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-bound} evaluates.  The time condition applies verbatim to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:path}, since the path is twice differentiable; but both
conditions are sufficient, not necessary, and
Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} documents for a twelve-site transverse-field Ising
chain that they overestimate the time and the step count actually needed by
several orders of magnitude --- in part because the Trotter error of a
digitized adiabatic sweep partly heals itself~\cite{Kovalsky2023}.  The
refinement times $T^{*}$ extracted below are therefore empirical, read off
the computed overlap with the exact fine ground state rather than from a
bound, and the product-formula step counts of Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized} are
chosen by the same exact benchmark.  The refinement circuit is optimizer-free
in execution --- no parameter is trained --- but its controls $(T,\mu,n_s)$
are calibrated here against the exact answer; an implementable calibration
would read them from the acceptance statistics of the filter or from a
coarse-space estimate of $\chi$, and we have not demonstrated that.
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-bound} evaluates both conditions for our path and
quantifies the overshoot.

\subsection{The rodeo algorithm}
\label{sec:rodeo}
The rodeo algorithm~\cite{Choi2021,Qian2024} removes the residual error left by the
adiabatic tail.  One cycle acts on the system register plus a single ancilla
(reused across cycles by mid-circuit measurement; the circuit is shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:circ-rodeo}): a Hadamard on the ancilla, the
controlled evolution $e^{-iH t_k}$ of the system, a phase gate $P(E\,t_k)$ on the
ancilla (which effectively shifts $H\to H-E$), a second Hadamard, and an ancilla
measurement.  Post-selecting the ancilla in $\ket{0}$ applies the non-unitary filter
$\tfrac12\big(\mathbb{1}+e^{iE t_k}e^{-iH t_k}\big)$ to the system: an eigenstate
$\ket{E_j}$ acquires the factor $e^{i(E-E_j)t_k/2}\cos[(E_j-E)t_k/2]$.  For an
eigenstate input the probability that all $M$ ancilla measurements return $0$ is
therefore~\cite{Qian2024}
\begin{equation}
  P_{0^{M}}\!\big(E\mid\{t_k\}\big)=\prod_{k=1}^{M}
  \cos^2\!\Big[\tfrac{t_k}{2}\big(\Eobj-E\big)\Big],
  \label{eq:rodeo1}
\end{equation}
and averaging the $t_k$ over a Gaussian of RMS width $\sigma$ gives
\begin{equation}
  P_{0^{M}}(E)=\Bigg[\frac{1+e^{-(\Eobj-E)^2\sigma^2/2}}{2}\Bigg]^{M}.
  \label{eq:rodeo2}
\end{equation}
On resonance ($E=\Eobj$) the success probability is unity at every cycle, while
off-resonant weight is suppressed geometrically toward the background $2^{-M}$ (the
arithmetic mean of $\cos^2$ is $1/2$).  Two readings of that background must
be kept apart: the Gaussian-averaged single-cycle factor
$q=\tfrac12[1+e^{-(\Eobj-E)^2\sigma^2/2}]$ is never smaller than $1/2$, so for
the \emph{ensemble} of random schedules $q^M\ge2^{-M}$ is a lower bound on the
retained fraction of any component, reached only far from resonance
($\abs{\Eobj-E}\sigma\gg1$); for one \emph{fixed} schedule there is no such
bound, since an individual cosine factor can vanish.  Scanning $E$ locates
eigenvalues as peaks whose width is $\sim1/\sigma$ at fixed $M$ and, since
$q(E)^M\simeq\exp[-M\sigma^2(E-E_j)^2/4]$ near a peak, $\sim1/(\sigma\sqrt M)$
when the cycle number is also varied; fixing $E$ at the target eigenvalue
turns the algorithm into a state-preparation filter.

The controlled evolution is the only system-size-dependent ingredient.  On hardware
$e^{-iH t}$ is realized by a Suzuki--Trotter decomposition over the $2k$
qubits~\cite{Trotter1959,Suzuki1976,Childs2021}; for the single-qubit benchmark of
Ref.~\cite{Qian2024} it reduces to a $Z$--$Y$--$Z$ Euler rotation
$R_{\hat n}(\theta)=e^{-i\theta\,\hat n\cdot\vec\sigma/2}$ implemented through the
Qiskit native gate.  In Secs.~\ref{sec:res-uccsd}--\ref{sec:res-rodeo} we apply the
controlled evolution exactly within the conserved $N$-particle sector to isolate the
algorithmic behavior; the circuit-level pipeline with explicit product formulas,
and the Trotter error it introduces, are quantified in Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized}.
Throughout we draw the cycle times $t_k$ from a Gaussian as in
Ref.~\cite{Choi2021}; optimized deterministic~\cite{Cohen2023} or
geometric~\cite{Patkowski2026} time schedules suppress off-resonant weight faster
at fixed runtime and would only improve the numbers reported here, as would the
controlled-reversal-gate compilation of the controlled evolution of
Ref.~\cite{BeeLindgren2022}.

\paragraph{Convergence criterion and the random-time ensemble.}
With $E$ tuned to the target eigenvalue $E_0$, the all-zero acceptance probability of
an input $\ket{\psi}=\sum_k c_k\ket{E_k}$ after $M$ cycles, averaged over the
Gaussian time sets, is
\begin{equation}
  P_M=p+\sum_{k\neq0}\abs{c_k}^2\,q_k^{M},
  \qquad p=\abs{c_0}^2 ,
  \label{eq:PM}
\end{equation}
with $q_k=\tfrac12\big(1+e^{-(E_k-E_0)^2\sigma^2/2}\big)$ the Gaussian-averaged
single-cycle factor of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rodeo2}.
On resonance and with exact evolution the target weight $p$ is preserved at
every round while off-resonant components decay geometrically, so
$P_M\to p$; at a detuning $d=E-E_0$ the retained target weight is
$p\,q_0(E)^M$ and decays with $M$, so a substantial acceptance requires
$\abs{d}\sigma\sqrt M\ll1$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-rodeo} tests this), and with a
product-formula evolution the preservation is only approximate
(Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized}).  For an exactly degenerate target energy the
filter selects the eigenspace, not a vector; and a component with zero
initial overlap cannot be created by any function of $H$.  Two fidelities
must be distinguished for a randomized filter, whose schedules are drawn
afresh for every attempted preparation.  For one fixed time set $\{t_k\}$ the post-selected state has
a fidelity $\mathcal F(\{t_k\})$ and an acceptance probability $P(\{t_k\})$; the
fidelity of the \emph{ensemble of accepted runs}, which is what repeated
executions on a device deliver, is the acceptance-weighted average
$\mathcal F_M=\langle P\,\mathcal F\rangle/\langle P\rangle$, and for it the
spectral decomposition gives exactly
\begin{equation}
  1-\mathcal{F}_M=\frac{\sum_{k\neq0}\abs{c_k}^2 q_k^{M}}
                       {p+\sum_{k\neq0}\abs{c_k}^2 q_k^{M}}
  \le\frac{(1-p)\,q_{\max}^{M}}{p+(1-p)\,q_{\max}^{M}},
  \label{eq:fid}
\end{equation}
with $q_{\max}=\tfrac12\big(1+e^{-\Delta_{\min}^2\sigma^2/2}\big)$ and
$\Delta_{\min}$ the smallest excitation energy carrying weight.  The
familiar estimate $1-\mathcal F_M\simeq\tfrac{1-p}{p}2^{-M}$ is the limit
$\Delta_{\min}\sigma\gg1$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fid}; for the pairing spectra used
below ($\Delta_{\min}\sigma\ge5$) the two are indistinguishable, and we plot
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fid} as the analytic expectation of the ensemble average.  All
sampled infidelities in this paper are acceptance-weighted; the unweighted
mean of $\mathcal F(\{t_k\})$ over time sets differs from $\mathcal F_M$ by at
most $1/p-1$ relative, i.e.\ less than one percent for inputs with $p>0.99$ and
a few percent for $p\simeq0.87$, and the dominant uncertainty of a sampled
point is instead the finite number of schedules (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-rodeo}).  Reaching an ensemble fidelity $1-\delta$ needs
$M\gtrsim\log_{1/q_{\max}}[(1-p)/(p\,\delta)]$ cycles, and since the expected
number of repetitions per accepted run is $1/P_M\to1/p$, the ideal
controlled-evolution effort at a resolution $\epsilon$ sufficient to isolate
the target scales as $O\!\big(\abs{\log\delta}/(p\,\epsilon)\big)$~\cite{Choi2021}.
This is not the gate complexity of the implemented pipeline: input
preparation, the search for the target energy, the accuracy of the
Hamiltonian simulation and the reduction of the product-formula step with the
requested precision are counted separately in Sec.~\ref{sec:circuits}, and
the accuracy floor found there is the direct reason for the distinction.  The filter is efficient
whenever $p$ is close to one, whatever supplies that overlap --- resolution
refinement, a variational circuit or a cheap correlated reference --- which is
why the comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:depth} is between \emph{inputs} to the
same filter.

\subsection{Coarse solver: UCCSD versus exact references}
\label{sec:res-uccsd}
Table~\ref{tab:uccsd} lists the ground-state energies at $g=1$ for $N=4$ as the basis
grows from $k=2$ to $k=8$.  The gate-level UCCSD circuit reproduces the operator-level
(statevector) UCCSD to machine precision and stays variational ($E_{\rm UCCSD}\ge
E_{\rm FCI}$), tracking FCI to $\sim10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$, whereas CCD over-binds.
Figure~\ref{fig:methods} compares all solvers (exact, CCD, statevector and gate-level
UCCSD, and a hardware-efficient ansatz~\cite{Kandala2017}) as a function of $k$.  The hardware-efficient
ansatz is exact at $k=2$ and reaches FCI at $k=3$ only after a multi-start
optimization, and struggles at $k=4$ --- the symmetry-blind landscape that motivates
the physically structured UCCSD circuit.  Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} shows that the same
UCCSD solver remains a tight variational bound all the way to $k=8$ (sixteen qubits),
at the cost of a CNOT count that grows steeply ($1312$ CNOTs already at $k=4$); the
compiled circuits and their depths are analyzed in Sec.~\ref{sec:circuits}.
Two remarks keep this table in proportion.  First, an energy error is not an
infidelity: for a normalized state $\expval{H}-E_0=\sum_{j>0}\abs{c_j}^2(E_j-E_0)
\ge\Delta\,(1-\mathcal F)$, so with the physical gap $\Delta=1.823$ the $k=4$
energy error $1.44\times10^{-3}$ already caps the infidelity at
$7.9\times10^{-4}$; the actual value, obtained from the optimized state, is
$1-\mathcal F_{\rm UCCSD}=1.78\times10^{-4}$, a factor eight below the energy
error, and it is this infidelity that every comparison below uses.  Second, the
scan in $k$ at fixed $N=4$ is a basis-size study --- the fixed-$N$ sector grows
as $k^{4}$ and the seniority-zero sector as $k^{2}$ --- and it concerns the
UCCSD solver alone, not the complete pipeline; it is not evidence of favorable
many-particle scaling at fixed filling.

\subsection{Resolution refinement: overlap and energy}
\label{sec:res-refine}
At $N=4$ the low-resolution ($k=2$) space is a single filled determinant, so the
prolonged state is the high-resolution HF reference, with first-guess overlaps
$0.917$ for refinement to $k=3$ and $0.868$ to $k=4$.  Adiabatic evolution along the
gapped path of Eq.~\eqref{eq:path} drives both quantities toward their exact targets:
the overlap rises to $\simeq0.999$ and the energy falls from $E_{\rm HF}=1$ to the FCI
value from above --- $0.7964$ for $k=3$ (FCI $0.794697$) and $0.6397$ for $k=4$ (FCI
$0.635548$) at $T=30$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:fourparticle}).  Energies above FCI
are guaranteed by the variational principle for any normalized state; the
improvement with $T$ is what is computed, and monotonicity at every time is
not implied.  The ``exact-slice'' propagation still approximates the
continuously changing Hamiltonian by $160$ midpoint slices; refining to
$1\,280$ slices changes the overlap by $1.5\times10^{-6}$ at $T=25$
($0.9975713\to0.9975698$) and by $1.1\times10^{-6}$ at $T=30$, far below the
adiabatic infidelities of order $10^{-3}$ discussed below.  The
instantaneous spectrum stays open along the whole path
(Fig.~\ref{fig:refine}, right), which makes adiabatic reasoning applicable
but does not by itself select the $T\sim1/\Delta E$ cost law discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:refine}; the larger $k=4$ space starts lower in overlap and
converges more slowly.

\subsection{A nontrivial coarse stage: the $k=3\to4$ chain}
\label{sec:res-k34}
At $N=4$ the $k=2$ sector is a single determinant, so in the chain above the
coarse ``solver'' is gate-free and the pipeline's first stage does no work.  To
exercise the pipeline with a working coarse stage we start instead from the
gate-level UCCSD state at $k=3$ --- a genuine $272$-CNOT variational preparation,
indistinguishable from FCI at this resolution (Table~\ref{tab:uccsd}) --- prolong
$3\to4$, and refine along the same path, with the shift $\mu$ now referenced to
the \emph{variational} coarse energy $\expval{\Hlow}{\psi_{\rm low}}$, the only
quantity a device can know.  Figure~\ref{fig:chains} compares the two chains.
The better coarse start raises the prolonged overlap from $0.868$ to $0.964$ and
reaches the target overlap $0.99$ at $T^{*}=8$ (overlap $0.9907$; the grid of
Fig.~\ref{fig:gapscan} gives $9$) instead of $T^{*}=16$.  The $k=3$ coarse
state is essentially exact ($1-F<10^{-9}$), so this chain exercises a working
coarse stage but does not test the pipeline against a non-negligible
coarse-solver error.  Whether the shorter sweep saves gates is decided by the
embedded coarse term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Aterm}, which is not free when the
coarse space is nontrivial.  For the $2\to4$ chain $A$ is a single four-qubit
conditional phase ($14$ CNOTs per application, Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized});
for the $3\to4$ chain it is the complete $k=3$ Hamiltonian conditioned on the
two appended qubits being empty, about $350$ CNOTs per application, more than
the $296$ CNOTs of the high-resolution pairing step itself.  Because
$\Hlow$ contains non-commuting terms, the counted circuit does not
exponentiate $A$ as one block but splits it into its own coarse groups (the
conditioned diagonal term and three conditioned pair-hopping terms), and we
validate the chain at that level in Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized}: $22$
second-order steps at $T=8$ give $p=0.9912$, so the $3\to4$ chain costs
$2\times22\times(296+350)+96\simeq2.9\times10^{4}$ CNOTs, the $96$ being the
coarse preparation (two pair rotations prepare the three-dimensional
seniority-zero coarse space exactly; the $272$-CNOT UCCSD template prepares
the same state).  At the step density of Table~\ref{tab:depth} the $2\to4$
chain needs $43$ steps for $T^{*}=16$, i.e.\
$2\times43\times(296+14)\simeq2.7\times10^{4}$ CNOTs.  The shorter adiabatic
time of the better start is thus offset by the cost of carrying the coarse
Hamiltonian along the path, and the two chains cost the same within $10\%$;
neither figure is an optimized minimum.  Starting higher in the hierarchy
remains the right choice when the prolonged overlap of the trivial start is
poor (the strong-coupling regime of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-gscan}), but it is not
a gate saving in itself.

\subsection{Coupling scan and the path gap}
\label{sec:res-gscan}
All results above sit at $g=1$; Fig.~\ref{fig:gapscan} probes the refinement
across couplings and tests the $T\sim1/\Delta E$ claim directly.  Scanning
$g=0.3$--$4$ at fixed $\mu=E_{\rm low}+0.6$ (left panel), the minimum
instantaneous gap along the path stays open and nearly constant --- it is set by
the shift itself, $\Delta E_{\min}\simeq\mu-E_{\rm low}$, dropping only to
$0.57$ at $g=4$ --- while the time to reach overlap $0.99$ grows from
$T^{*}=1$ at $g=0.3$ to $T^{*}=65$ at $g=4$.  The growth goes together with
the loss of initial overlap of the prolonged state, which falls from $0.99$
to $0.38$ over the same range, consistent with a larger change of the
eigenstate along the path; but changing $g$ also changes the transition
matrix elements and the eigenvector path, so the scan does not isolate the
overlap as the cause (the shift scan below changes $T^{*}$ at fixed initial
overlap).  It does establish that the nearly constant minimum gap does not
determine the required time by itself, which identifies the practical
limiter at strong coupling as the starting state rather than a closing gap;
whether a coarse start higher in the hierarchy (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-k34}) is
then the better route is a question of total cost, including the coarse
preparation and its conditioned Hamiltonian, not of sweep time alone.

Because the path gap is controlled by the shift, varying $\mu$ at fixed $g=1$
changes the minimum gap over a factor five (right panel).  The four finite
threshold times, $(\Delta E_{\min},T^{*})=(0.3,50)$, $(0.6,16)$, $(1.0,8)$,
$(1.5,6)$, are fitted with an intercept by $T^{*}\approx17.1/\Delta E_{\min}-8.4$
(sum of squared residuals $28$) and, equally legitimately, by
$T^{*}\approx4.1/\Delta E_{\min}^{2}+4.2$ (residuals $0.2$); four threshold
times read off a coarse time grid, along a family of paths whose operator
changes with $\mu$, do not identify an exponent, and in particular do not
exclude inverse-square behavior.  We therefore present the scan as a
sensitivity study.  The response norm $\chi$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:refine} makes
the limitation explicit (Fig.~\ref{fig:assess}, right).  With
$u=\sin^2(\pi s/2)$ the path is $H(u)=(1-u)A+uB$ with
$A=-b\ket{\rm HF}\!\bra{\rm HF}$, $b=\mu-E_{\rm low}$, and at $u=0$ the HF
determinant couples through $\partial_uH$ to four pair-transfer
configurations with amplitude $-g/2$, all at excitation energy $b$, so
$\chi_u(0)=g^2/b^2$ exactly: $11.1$ at $b=0.3$ and $0.44$ at $b=1.5$, a
factor $25$, and the maximum of $\chi_u(s)$ sits at $s=0$ for every shift.
The quantity that the inverse-gap argument of Ref.~\cite{Bogner2026} assumes
uniformly bounded is thus itself varied by the shift scan, which cannot then
test that argument by attributing the timing changes to the gap alone.  Two
further gaps should be kept apart from the artificial shift-controlled gap
at $s=0$: the physical gap of $\Hhigh$ in the $N=4$ sector ($1.823$ at
$g=1$), a conservative all-sector bound, and the dynamically accessible
seniority-zero excitation gap at $s=1$ ($2.300$), which is the one the
transition matrix elements see.  The same scan doubles as the sensitivity
study for the shift: the choice $\mu_{\rm buf}=0.6$ used throughout is
conservative, and enlarging the buffer to $1.5$ \emph{shortens} the
refinement to $T^{*}=6$.  For a one-dimensional coarse space there is no
upper limit to the buffer other than the growth of the energy scale (and
hence of the Trotter error) of the path; for coarse spaces with several
states the useful initial gap saturates at the coarse excitation gap, as
noted in Sec.~\ref{sec:refine}.  Section~\ref{sec:depth} adds the limiting
case of this observation: an ordinary adiabatic ramp from the diagonal
(HF) Hamiltonian, which has no shift and whose seniority-zero gap at $s=0$
is $2.0$, refines the same determinant in a quarter of the time.

\subsection{Sufficient conditions versus practice}
\label{sec:res-bound}
The conditions quoted in Sec.~\ref{sec:refine} can be evaluated exactly for
the path of Eq.~\eqref{eq:path}.  Writing $A=P(\Hlow-\mu)P^\dagger$ and
$B=\Hhigh-\mu$, the schedule gives
$\dot H=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sin(\pi s)\,(B-A)$ and
$\ddot H=\tfrac{\pi^2}{2}\cos(\pi s)\,(B-A)$, so the integrand of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Tbound} is fixed by the single number $\norm{B-A}$ and by the
instantaneous gap $\Delta(s)$, both taken in the $N=4$ sector in which the
evolution takes place.  Figure~\ref{fig:bound} shows the two along the
$k=2\to4$ path: the gap rises from the shift-controlled $0.600$ at $s=0$ to
the physical gap of $\Hhigh$ at $s=1$, and the integrand peaks near
$s\simeq0.25$, where $\sin(\pi s)$ is already sizable while the gap is still
close to its minimum; the term $7\norm{\dot H}^2/\Delta^3$ supplies $94\%$ of
the integral.  Table~\ref{tab:bound} collects the numbers.  Here
$\norm{B-A}=7.62$ and $\int_0^1[\cdots]\,ds=511$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Tbound} demands $T\ge5.1\times10^{3}$ for an infidelity of
$10^{-2}$ (and $1.6\times10^{4}$ for $10^{-3}$), whereas the computed overlap
reaches $0.99$ at $T^{*}=16$: the sufficient condition overshoots the
practical time by a factor $3\times10^{2}$.  The step-count condition is
looser still.  With $\Lambda_1=\max_s\norm{\dot H}=12.0$ and, for the term
partition of the gate compilation of Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized} (the
conditioned coarse term, the diagonal term and the six pair-hopping terms),
$\Lambda_2=\max_s\sum_{i<j}\norm{[H_i(s),H_j(s)]}=13.0$, the first-order
bound at $\eta=\sqrt{\epsilon}/2$ requires $3.4\times10^{4}$ steps along the
$T^{*}=16$ path --- and $3.4\times10^{9}$ along the path of length $T_{\rm b}$
--- while $160$ first-order or $40$ second-order steps already reproduce the
exact-slice overlap at $T^{*}$ to within the adiabatic error of the path
itself.  The two step counts answer different questions and the ratio
between them must be read with that in mind: the bound certifies an
operator-norm error of the digitized unitary, whereas reproducing one
scalar overlap does not certify that the two output vectors, let alone the
two unitaries, are close.  (The companion paper~\cite{PHcompanion} repeats this evaluation
with the particle-hole term switched on, where the time bound is insensitive
to the pair breaking because the minimum gap is set by the shift, while the
step bound grows with the number of Trotter groups.)

These ratios --- $3\times10^{2}$ for the time and $2\times10^{2}$ for the
step count --- are the fermionic counterpart of what
Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} reports for nucleation of a twelve-site Ising chain
($6\times10^{1}$ and $10^{7}$, respectively), and they carry the same
message: the conditions certify convergence but do not predict cost.  Part
of the reason is visible in the integrand.  The bound pays for the full
operator norm $\norm{B-A}=7.62$ --- the same value in the full $N=4$ sector
and in the six-dimensional seniority-zero block that the dynamics
actually explores, so it is not an artifact of inaccessible sectors --- and
for the global gap, whereas the adiabatic error is governed by the
individual matrix elements of $dH/ds$ between the tracked state and the
states it couples to, relative to their individual gaps.  Those elements are
small but not zero for the highest accessible states: at $s=0.25$ the
derivative couplings from the ground state to the five seniority-zero
excited states are $0.60$, $0.37$, $0.00$, $0.11$ and $0.08$.  A global
norm therefore overestimates the cost by ignoring the state-specific
structure of the couplings; the magnitude of the overshoot alone does not
single out one cause, nor does it verify the inverse-gap mechanism of
Ref.~\cite{Bogner2026}.

\subsection{Rodeo filtering of the refined state}
\label{sec:res-rodeo}
We verified the rodeo primitive in two ways: the explicit ancilla circuit agrees with
the compact filter $\tfrac12(\mathbb{1}+e^{iEt}e^{-iHt})$ to $10^{-16}$, and the
all-zero probabilities reproduce Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rodeo1}--\eqref{eq:rodeo2}, with the
Gaussian-averaged single-cycle factor matching $\tfrac12(1+e^{-(\Delta E\,\sigma)^2/2})$
to three digits.

The state fed to the filter is the resolution-refined $k=4$ state of
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-refine} at $T=25$ with exact slices and exact controlled
evolution, with overlap $p=0.9976$ with the exact ground state; for contrast
we also use the unrefined prolonged guess $\Phi_0$ ($p=0.868$).  This is an
\emph{ideal} operating point, chosen to exhibit the filter; the digital
pipeline of Sec.~\ref{sec:circuits} uses other refinement times and a
product-formula evolution, and Table~\ref{tab:ops} keeps the two apart.
Scanning the target energy $E$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:scan}; $M=4$ cycles, $12$ time sets
per energy) produces a peak at the ground-state energy; a coarse scan
($\sigma=2$, $60$ energies on $[-0.5,4]$) resolves it from the excited band,
and a fine scan ($\sigma=8$, $161$ energies with spacing $0.0075$ on a
window of width $1.2$ centered on the highest point of the coarse scan,
$E=0.644$, i.e.\ chosen without knowledge of $E_0$) locates it at
$E=0.6362$ against the exact $E_0=0.635548$ by three-point parabolic
interpolation at the grid maximum.  On a device every acceptance is a shot
estimate: adding binomial noise for $10^{3}$ and $10^{4}$ shots per energy
and locating the peak by a quadratic fit through the points above $80\%$ of
the maximum gives $0.637\pm0.001$ and $0.6373\pm0.0004$, so with this scan
the systematic error of the peak estimator ($\sim2\times10^{-3}$, from the
non-parabolic shape of the peak over the fitted window) exceeds the shot
noise, and the agreement to $10^{-3}$ is a property of the scan design
rather than a demonstrated four-decimal precision.  Starting from the
refined state, essentially all spectral weight sits in the single
ground-state peak; starting from $\Phi_0$, the small residual weight on
excited states is visible as secondary structure above the $2^{-M}$
background.  The scan is part of the cost of the method: the fine scan alone
evaluates $161$ energies with $12$ time sets each, i.e.\ about $2\times10^{3}$
four-cycle runs, each repeated over enough shots to estimate an acceptance
probability.

Fixing $E=E_0$ and applying $M$ cycles with $\sigma=3$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence}; $40$ Gaussian time sets, the same sets for both
inputs, generator seed $7$ reset per input), the fidelity rises geometrically
and the cumulative acceptance settles at $\approx p$, in agreement with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:PM}--\eqref{eq:fid}.  For the refined input the exact
Gaussian-ensemble infidelity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fid} is $1.2\times10^{-3}$
after one cycle, $6.1\times10^{-4}$ after two, $3.8\times10^{-5}$ after six
and $2.4\times10^{-6}$ after ten, and $12$ cycles are needed to reach
$10^{-6}$; the $40$-schedule sample gives $1.35\times10^{-3}$,
$6.5\times10^{-4}$, $2.1\times10^{-5}$ and $2.0\times10^{-6}$.  The sample
lies $45\%$ below the ensemble value at six cycles, which is ordinary finite
sampling of products of random attenuation factors: the relative standard
error of a $40$-schedule mean of the residual weight, computed from
$2\times10^{4}$ schedules, is $13\%$ at $M=2$, $37\%$ at $M=6$ and $82\%$ at
$M=10$ (the error bars of Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence}), so the sampled points
are statistically consistent with the curve but carry no precision at the
smallest infidelities, and the quoted infidelities of this paper are the
ensemble values unless stated otherwise.  (The acceptance weighting itself
changes the sampled infidelity by at most $1/p-1=0.24\%$ at this $p$; an
earlier version of the notebook advanced one generator through both inputs
and reported $5.5\times10^{-6}$ at ten cycles, an equally valid sample.)  The
prolonged input reaches $1.3\times10^{-4}$ after ten cycles (ensemble value
$1.5\times10^{-4}$) while its acceptance holds at $0.868$.  Each cycle buys
roughly a factor two in infidelity, so reaching $10^{-6}$ from $p=0.998$
takes of order ten cycles, not one or two.  Two practical checks complete
the picture.  First, the target energy on a device is the scan estimate, not
the exact eigenvalue: repeating the filter at the scan estimate
($E-E_0=7\times10^{-4}$, $\abs{d}\sigma\sqrt M=0.005$ at six cycles)
changes no digit of the ensemble infidelities quoted above.  Second, a
deliberately detuned target attenuates the ground state as well: for
$E-E_0=0.05$, $0.1$ and $0.2$ the six-cycle ensemble infidelity is unchanged
within $3\%$, $14\%$ and a factor $1.7$, while the acceptance falls from
$0.998$ to $0.965$, $0.873$ and $0.596$ (exact ensemble values, Sec.~19 of
the notebook; an earlier version quoted sampled values) --- in
this regime the acceptance, not the fidelity, is the sensitive diagnostic of
a wrong target, and it then no longer tends to the input overlap; a detuning
toward another eigenvalue would instead select that state, which the
acceptance alone does not certify against.

\section{Quantum circuits and depth comparison}
\label{sec:circuits}
% ===========================================================================
All quantum operations in this work are compiled to a common gate set of
single-qubit rotations and CNOTs, so the resource costs of the different routes
to the high-resolution ground state --- a full-space UCCSD--VQE, the
refinement-plus-filter pipeline, and the filter applied directly to cheaper
inputs --- can be compared gate by gate.  Every number quoted below is produced
in the accompanying notebook.  Two levels of simulation must be distinguished.
The Pauli-rotation primitive, the UCCSD excitation circuits, the
hardware-efficient ansatz and the ancilla-based rodeo cycle are simulated
\emph{gate by gate} on the full $2k$-qubit register and verified against exact
exponentials.  The product-formula evolutions of Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized},
by contrast, are simulated \emph{block by block}: each term of the Hamiltonian
splitting is a set of mutually commuting Pauli strings whose exponential is
realized exactly by the corresponding rotation--CNOT block, and we apply that
exact block exponential within the conserved $N$-particle sector.  This
reproduces faithfully the state that the compiled circuit would produce, and
the CNOT counts are those of the compiled blocks, but it does not execute
every individual rotation and CNOT; we therefore quote CNOT \emph{counts}
rather than scheduled circuit depths, which depend on the connectivity and
parallelism of a device.

\subsection{Circuit building blocks}
\label{sec:blocks}
The elementary primitive is the Pauli rotation $e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}P}$ for a
Pauli string $P=\bigotimes_q\sigma_q$~\cite{NielsenChuang}: each active qubit is rotated into the $Z$
eigenbasis ($H$ for $X$, $HS^\dagger$ for $Y$), a CNOT ladder accumulates the
parity onto the last active qubit, a single $R_z(\phi)$ is applied, and the
ladder and basis changes are uncomputed.  A string of weight $w$ therefore costs
$2(w-1)$ CNOTs and one parametrized rotation; the circuit reproduces the exact
exponential to $3\times10^{-16}$.

\paragraph{UCCSD preparation circuit.}
Through the Jordan--Wigner map a single excitation compiles into $2$ Pauli
rotations whose weight grows with the distance between the spin orbitals
($8$ CNOTs for adjacent levels, $16$ for the $0\!\to\!4$ single at $k=3$), and a
generic double excitation into $8$ rotations ($48$--$64$ CNOTs).  The doubles
that dominate the pairing problem are special: for a time-reversed pair moving
between levels $p$ and $q$ the operators act on the adjacent qubits
$(2p,2p{+}1)$ and $(2q,2q{+}1)$, so the internal Jordan--Wigner $Z$ strings
cancel and all $8$ Pauli strings have weight $4$ --- $48$ CNOTs per pair-hopping
term \emph{independent of the level separation}.  The full state preparation at
$N=4$, $g=1$ then costs $8$ excitation operators, $40$ rotations and $272$ CNOTs
at $k=3$, and $26$ operators, $160$ rotations and $1312$ CNOTs at $k=4$
(Table~\ref{tab:uccsd}); at $k=2$ the ansatz is empty (the sector is a single
determinant) and the preparation is gate-free.

Figure~\ref{fig:circ-vqe} shows both levels of this hierarchy.  Panel (a) is
the VQE at the ansatz level: the product of excitation unitaries acts on the HF
register, the energy is estimated from repeated measurements, and a classical
optimizer updates the $P$ amplitudes $\boldsymbol\theta$ before the circuit is
run again --- it is this feedback loop, wrapped around the full-depth state
preparation, that multiplies the $1312$-CNOT cost into the total gate budget of
the traditional route.  Panel (b) is the compiled interior of a single
weight-$4$ Pauli rotation, the sub-block that occurs eight times in every
pair-hopping double and $160$ times in the $k=4$ preparation; its only
parametrized element is the central $R_z(\phi)$, with $\phi$ fixed by the
amplitude of the parent excitation operator.

\paragraph{Hardware-efficient ansatz.}
The HEA~\cite{Kandala2017} used for contrast consists of $L$ layers of
$R_y$ rotations on all $2k$ qubits interleaved with a CNOT entangler
(nearest-neighbor pairs plus a wrap-around bond), i.e.\ $2k(L{+}1)$ variational
angles and only $O(2kL)$ CNOTs: $12$ angles at $k=2$ ($L=2$) and $30$ at $k=3$
($L=4$).  Its depth is far smaller than UCCSD, but the circuit carries no
particle-number or seniority structure; a number-penalty term and multi-start
optimization are needed at $k=3$, and at $k=4$ the optimizer fails to reach the
ground state (Fig.~\ref{fig:methods}).  Depth is not the only currency:
trainability of the symmetry-blind landscape becomes the bottleneck first.

\paragraph{Rodeo cycle.}
One rodeo cycle (Fig.~\ref{fig:circ-rodeo}) adds a Hadamard on the ancilla, the
controlled evolution $e^{-iHt_k}$, the ancilla phase $P(Et_k)$ --- the element
that effectively shifts $H\to H-E$ --- a second Hadamard, and a mid-circuit
measurement; the single ancilla is reset and reused across the $M$ cycles.  The
contrast with Fig.~\ref{fig:circ-vqe}(a) is the structural point of this
section: the rodeo circuit contains \emph{no} variational parameters and closes
no optimization loop.  The times $t_k$ are drawn once from a Gaussian, the
target energy $E$ is a classical setting, and the algorithm is driven entirely
by the measurement record --- a run is accepted when all $M$ ancilla outcomes
read $0$, which applies the filter of Sec.~\ref{sec:rodeo} cycle by cycle.
Where the VQE re-executes its full-depth circuit to steer $\boldsymbol\theta$,
the rodeo circuit is executed as printed, and its only system-size-dependent
element is the controlled evolution.  The controlled evolution is
compiled from the same Pauli-rotation primitive: in each rotation sub-circuit
only the central $R_z$ needs to be controlled (if the rotation is absent the
basis changes and CNOT ladder cancel), and a controlled $R_z$ costs $2$ extra
CNOTs.  For the pairing Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:H} a first-order Trotter step
contains the $k(k{-}1)/2$ pair-hopping terms ($8$ weight-$4$ rotations, $48$
CNOTs each), $k$ diagonal $ZZ$ rotations ($2$ CNOTs each) and $2k$ single-qubit
phases, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  N_{\rm CNOT}^{\rm step}(k)=24\,k(k-1)+2k
  \label{eq:trottercost}
\end{equation}
CNOTs uncontrolled --- $296$ at $k=4$ --- and $\approx 4.2\times10^{2}$ CNOTs per
step once the controls are added.  A rodeo cycle with $n_T$ Trotter steps
therefore costs $\approx 4.2\times10^{2}\,n_T$ CNOTs at $k=4$.

\subsection{The Trotterized pipeline}
\label{sec:trotterized}
The results of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-rodeo} used exact sector exponentials; on
hardware both the refinement slices and the controlled evolution are product
formulas, and their error must be quantified at the circuit level.  We split the
Hamiltonian exactly as the gate compilation does: a diagonal term $D$ (kinetic
energy plus the $p{=}q$ pairing term; single-qubit phases and $ZZ$ rotations)
and one pair-hopping term $h_{pq}$ per level pair.  The eight Pauli strings of
each $h_{pq}$ commute, so every factor $e^{-ih_{pq}\delta t}$ is realized
\emph{exactly} by its $48$-CNOT block, and the only Trotter error is the
non-commutativity between terms --- exactly as on a device.  At $k=4$, $t=1$ the
second-order (symmetric) formula reaches spectral-norm error $3.4\times10^{-2}$
with $4$ steps and $8.3\times10^{-3}$ with $8$.

\paragraph{The embedded coarse term.}
The refinement path, Eq.~\eqref{eq:path}, contains a third kind of factor,
$e^{-i\cos^2(\pi s/2)A\,\delta t}$ with $A=P(\Hlow-\mu)P^\dagger$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Aterm}.  Gate-free prolongation does not make this evolution
gate-free.  For the $2\to4$ chain at $N=4$ the coarse space is the single
determinant $\ket{\rm HF}$, so $A=(E_{\rm low}-\mu)\ket{\rm HF}\!\bra{\rm HF}
=-0.6\,\ket{\rm HF}\!\bra{\rm HF}$, and within the $N=4$ sector $\ket{\rm HF}$ is
the unique state with the four appended qubits $4$--$7$ all in $\ket0$.  Its
exponential is therefore a phase conditioned on four qubits being empty,
$X^{\otimes4}\,C^{3}P(\phi)\,X^{\otimes4}$ with $\phi=0.6\cos^2(\pi s/2)\delta t$.
Expanding the projector, $\ket{1111}\!\bra{1111}=2^{-4}\sum_{S}(-1)^{|S|}Z_S$
over the $15$ nonempty subsets $S$ of the four qubits, gives a phase polynomial
of $15$ commuting $Z$-strings; ordered along a Gray code it compiles into
$14$ CNOTs and $15$ $R_z$ rotations (the naive ladder-per-string construction
costs $34$ CNOTs).  We verified that this operator reproduces the sector
matrix exponential of $A$ exactly.  For a chain that starts from a nontrivial
coarse space the term is $(\Hlow-\mu)$ on the coarse register conditioned on
the appended qubits being empty; for the $3\to4$ chain each of the $33$ Pauli
rotations of a $k=3$ step acquires a double control ($6$ CNOTs per rotation
with a three-qubit Gray-code phase polynomial), for about $350$ CNOTs per
application, which is the figure used in Sec.~\ref{sec:res-k34}.  All
refinement budgets below include the coarse term; for the $2\to4$ chain it
adds $14$ CNOTs to the $296$ of a first-order step, i.e.\ $2\times80\times14
=2\,240$ CNOTs or $5\%$ over the $T=30$ path.

Figure~\ref{fig:trotter} shows the two pipeline stages under this compilation.
For the refinement (left), $n_s=80$ second-order steps along the $T=30$ path
reproduce the exact-slice result to within its own adiabatic error, delivering
$p=0.9977$ against the exact-slice $0.9988$; first-order steps need several
hundred.  For the rodeo stage (right) the honest scheme fixes the maximum
Trotter step \emph{size} $\delta t$, so a cycle with Gaussian time $t_k$ uses
$n_k=\lceil \abs{t_k}/\delta t\rceil$ steps of size $t_k/n_k$
($\sigma\sqrt{2/\pi}/\delta t\approx13$ steps on average at $\sigma=4$,
$\delta t=0.25$).  The filter is strikingly diagnostic of its own Trotter
error: at $\delta t=1$ the corrupted evolution is largely \emph{rejected} ---
the acceptance collapses to $0.10$ after six cycles and the fidelity does not
approach the target accuracy in the displayed range --- while at
$\delta t=0.25$ the infidelity tracks the exact-evolution curve down to
$1.2\times10^{-4}$ at $M=6$ with acceptance $0.989$.  The intermediate
$\delta t=0.5$ settles at a floor of $2\times10^{-3}$.  The precise
statement behind these floors is the following.  With exact evolution on
resonance every post-selection operator $K_t=\tfrac12(\mathbb1+e^{iE_0t}e^{-iHt})$
leaves the ground-state amplitude unchanged and all $K_t$ share the
eigenvectors of $H$, so the accepted ensemble converges to the ground state
and the acceptance to $p$.  With the product-formula evolution $\tilde U(t)$
the operators $\tilde K_t=\tfrac12(\mathbb1+e^{iE_0t}\tilde U(t))$ no longer
preserve the true ground state, and since the schedules are drawn afresh
for every attempt the accepted ensemble after $M$ cycles is
$\rho_M=\mathcal K^M(\rho_{\rm in})/\Tr\mathcal K^M(\rho_{\rm in})$ with the
averaged map $\mathcal K(\rho)=\mathbb E_t[\tilde K_t\rho\tilde K_t^\dagger]$.
The long-run state is the dominant eigenoperator of this trace-decreasing map
--- not the ground state of one effective Hamiltonian, and not a mixture of
the separate filters' ground states --- and its eigenvalue is the long-run
acceptance per cycle.  Evaluating $\mathcal K$ in the six-dimensional
seniority-zero block by quadrature over the half-Gaussian cycle times
(notebook, Sec.~19) gives an asymptotic infidelity of $1.2\times10^{-4}$ at
$\delta t=0.25$ with eigenvalue $0.9979$, $1.5\times10^{-3}$ at
$\delta t=0.5$ with eigenvalue $0.966$, and $0.21$ at $\delta t=1$ with
eigenvalue $0.64$; these reproduce the floors of Fig.~\ref{fig:trotter} and
show that the long-run acceptance of the digital filter decays geometrically,
by $0.2\%$ per cycle at $\delta t=0.25$, rather than tending to $p$ as the
ideal theorem states.  Coherent product-formula errors thus both shift the
located energy and bias the prepared state; the rejection of grossly
inaccurate evolutions seen at $\delta t=1$ is a useful diagnostic, not a
guarantee that smaller coherent errors are removed.

\subsection{Gate budget at matched fidelity: which input should the filter get?}
\label{sec:depth}
The question that decides the value of the refinement stage is not whether the
pipeline beats an \emph{unfiltered} variational state --- a few rodeo cycles
improve any input with large overlap --- but whether refinement followed by
the filter beats the same filter applied to the best input that is available
cheaply, or no filter at all.  We therefore feed one and the same Trotterized
filter (second order, $\delta t=0.25$, $\sigma=4$, $\abs{t_k}$ drawn from a
Gaussian, $40$ common time sets drawn afresh per attempted preparation) eight
inputs: (i) the prolonged $k=2$ determinant, $p=0.868$, gate-free; (ii) the
four-pair restricted ansatz of Sec.~\ref{sec:adapt}, $p=0.99982$, $192$
CNOTs; (iii) the full-template gate-level UCCSD state, the same state for
$1\,312$ CNOTs; (iv) the prolonged $k=3$ coarse state of
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-k34}, $p=0.964$, prepared with two pair rotations ($96$
CNOTs) and fed to the filter \emph{without} refinement; (v) an ordinary
adiabatic ramp from the diagonal Hamiltonian,
$H(s)=D+\sin^2(\pi s/2)\sum_{pq}h_{pq}$ started from the HF determinant,
which has no shift and no coarse term and whose seniority-zero gap at $s=0$
is $2.0$: it reaches $p=0.9987$ at $T=4$ with exact slices and $p=0.9978$
with $11$ second-order steps, $2\times11\times296=6\,512$ CNOTs; (vi) the
Trotterized refined state at $T^{*}=16$ ($43$ second-order steps,
$p=0.9922$, $2\times43\times310=26\,660$ CNOTs including the coarse term);
(vii) the refined state at $T=30$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:trotter} ($80$ steps,
$p=0.9977$, $49\,600$ CNOTs); and (viii) the $3\to4$ chain of
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-k34} at the counted circuit level ($T=8$, $22$ steps with
the coarse term split into its coarse groups, $p=0.9912$, $28\,520$ CNOTs).
Inputs (iv) and (v) are the two baselines needed to isolate what the
specific refinement path contributes: (iv) asks whether refinement is needed
after a useful coarse solution, (v) whether the shift-controlled projector
path does anything that generic adiabatic preconditioning from the same
determinant does not.  For each input and each cycle number $M=0,\ldots,6$
we track the ensemble infidelity of the accepted runs, the acceptance $P_M$,
the CNOTs of one attempt that runs all $M$ cycles,
$C^{\rm att}_M=C_{\rm prep}+\sum_{m\le M}\langle c_m\rangle$ --- the coherent
two-qubit depth proxy of a successful run --- and the expected CNOTs per
accepted preparation,
\begin{equation}
  C_M=\frac{C_{\rm prep}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\big\langle P_{m-1}\,c_m\big\rangle}{\langle P_M\rangle},
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $c_m=2\times416\,n_m$ is the cost of the $m$-th controlled cycle, the
expectation is over the random schedules, and the factor $P_{m-1}$ inside it
accounts for early termination of a run at the first ancilla outcome $1$
(without early termination the numerator is simply
$C_{\rm prep}+\sum_m\langle c_m\rangle$; the difference is below $5\%$ for
all inputs).  Rejected attempts add to the total work but not to the
coherent depth of the accepted state.  Equation~\eqref{eq:cost} counts
repeated state preparation only.  One-time costs are reported separately
and are not comparable across routes without a shot model: the UCCSD
training took eight L-BFGS iterations in a noiseless simulation, each
requiring the energy and its gradient --- a hardware shot budget would need
an estimator, a grouping scheme, a shot precision and a stopping criterion
--- and the energy search of the rodeo stage costs the scans of
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-rodeo}, of order $10^{3}$ four-cycle runs times their
shots, with a preparation cost and peak statistics that depend on which
input is scanned.  For $Q$ requested accepted states the natural total is
$C_{\rm setup}+Q\,C_M$, and the setup is amortized away for large $Q$.

Figure~\ref{fig:baselines} and Table~\ref{tab:depth} give the result.  The
first observation is that the high-overlap inputs approach a common
product-formula-limited accuracy, $1-\mathcal F\simeq1.1$--$1.7\times10^{-4}$
after six cycles, the floor of the averaged post-selection map at
$\delta t=0.25$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized}); the determinant, which still
carries substantial excited weight at $M=6$ ($1.8\times10^{-3}$), and the
prolonged $k=3$ state ($7.6\times10^{-4}$) need more cycles to reach that
regime, and six-cycle values are not measured asymptotes.  The second
observation is the ranking at \emph{matched accuracy}, read off the minimum
cost over inputs and cycle numbers, $M=0$ included, for a target
infidelity: for every target above the floor --- $2\times10^{-3}$,
$5\times10^{-4}$ and $2\times10^{-4}$ --- the cheapest route is the
unfiltered four-pair ansatz, $192$ CNOTs at $1.8\times10^{-4}$, followed by
the same state in its $1\,312$-CNOT template; the cheapest route through the
filter is the HF ramp, with $1.7\times10^{4}$, $4.0\times10^{4}$ and
$6.4\times10^{4}$ CNOTs per accepted preparation after one, three and five
cycles; and the cheapest refinement route, the $T^{*}=16$ chain, costs
$4.9\times10^{4}$, $7.4\times10^{4}$ and $9.4\times10^{4}$ after two, four
and six cycles.  Refinement followed by the filter is therefore tied within a
factor $1.5$ with filtering the unrefined determinant (which reaches
$1.8\times10^{-3}$ after six cycles for $7.0\times10^{4}$ CNOTs), costs
$2.5$--$3$ times more than the plain adiabatic ramp at every target, and
costs $200$--$500$ times more than the four-pair preparation, which needs no
filter; the prolonged $k=3$ coarse state filtered directly reaches
$7.6\times10^{-4}$ after six cycles for $6.7\times10^{4}$ CNOTs, cheaper
than refining it first.  The cost ratios quoted in an earlier version of
this work at a fixed cycle number compared states at different achieved
errors and are superseded by these matched-accuracy figures; none of the
operating points is a globally optimized minimum, since refinement time,
shift and step size were not searched.  The claim that the hybrid undercuts
the variational preparation by orders of magnitude, which compared the UCCSD
\emph{energy error} with the pipeline \emph{infidelity}, does not survive the
comparison of like with like.

Table~\ref{tab:ops} records the operating points behind the different
fidelities quoted in this paper, which are not contradictory because they
belong to different stages of idealization: the ideal filter of
Sec.~\ref{sec:res-rodeo} and the digital pipeline of this section differ in
refinement time, slice treatment, filter width and evolution, and the residual
errors of the former are not achieved by the counted circuits of the latter.

What remains in favor of the refinement stage is qualitative but real.  It
contains no variational parameters: nothing is trained at full resolution,
no expectation values are measured during state preparation, and the only
classical inputs are the shift $\mu$, the schedule $T$ and the step count
$n_s$ --- which, as noted in Sec.~\ref{sec:refine}, were calibrated here
against the exact answer rather than from coarse-space information.  Its
gate count is dominated by the refinement, which scales with the Trotter
step of the fine Hamiltonian and with $T^{*}$, and is independent of how hard
the variational landscape is.  Whether this trade --- trainability for depth
--- pays off is a question about regimes in which the full-space variational
input is poor or expensive to train.  For the pairing model with $N=4$ that
regime is not reached at any size we can validate exactly
(Sec.~\ref{sec:sizes}); with four pairs on eight levels it is
(Sec.~\ref{sec:large}), and there refinement plus filter beats the
variational route, while the ramp baseline adds that, where an adiabatic
stage is wanted, the shift-controlled path is never the cheapest one; we
state the latter as the main negative finding of the present assessment.

\subsection{Remarks on adaptive and improved VQE}
\label{sec:adapt}
The steep UCCSD gate count is not irreducible, and much recent work aims
precisely at shallower variational circuits (see Ref.~\cite{Tilly2022} for a
review).  ADAPT-VQE~\cite{Grimsley2019} grows the ansatz one operator at a
time, at each step appending the pool operator with the largest energy gradient
and re-optimizing all amplitudes; it typically reaches a target accuracy with a
small fraction of the full UCCSD operator count and correspondingly shallower
circuits.  The pairing model makes the mechanism transparent: because the
Hamiltonian conserves seniority and the reference is seniority-zero, the
gradients of all singles and of all pair-breaking doubles vanish identically
on the seniority-zero manifold, so a selection by gradient would start from
the $\frac{N}{2}(k-\frac{N}{2})$ pair-hopping doubles.  We have
\emph{demonstrated} the corresponding restricted ansatz
(Sec.~\ref{sec:depth}): the four occupied-pair$\to$virtual-pair rotations of
the pool, in the order of the full ansatz, optimized on their own, give
$E=0.636987$ and infidelity $1.78\times10^{-4}$ --- the full $26$-operator
UCCSD result to all displayed digits, whose other $22$ amplitudes are
exactly zero at the optimum --- for $4\times48=192$ CNOTs in place of
$1\,312$.  This is an explicit restricted variational calculation, not an
ADAPT run: the symmetry selection rule says which gradients vanish, not that
ADAPT selects exactly four operators, each once, nor what error it reaches,
and it says nothing about unconstrained optima of an ordered ansatz.
Qubit-ADAPT-VQE~\cite{Tang2021} pushes further by drawing the pool from
individual Pauli strings, removing the eightfold string overhead per
fermionic double.  These developments are complementary to the present
pipeline rather than competing with it: the filter is agnostic to how its
input is produced, so an ADAPT-style state --- at the coarse or at the full
resolution --- slots in directly.  An ADAPT-VQE comparator proper remains
the natural next step of the assessment.  Conversely, no variational
compression by itself supplies the geometric, optimizer-free convergence that
the rodeo filter provides once the overlap is large.  A different route to a
shallower circuit --- keeping the Trotterized refinement path as the ansatz
structure and optimizing a few of its layers, as in the optimized nucleation
of Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} --- is taken up in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{A larger model space: four pairs on eight levels}
\label{sec:large}
The sizes of Table~\ref{tab:sizes} keep the variational input good and the
filter cycles cheap.  To test whether a larger and more correlated model
space changes the ranking, we repeat the matched comparison for $N=8$
particles on $k=8$ levels --- four pairs, sixteen qubits, the seniority-zero
sector of $\binom{8}{4}=70$ states inside the $12\,870$-dimensional $N=8$
sector --- for $g=0.5$ to $2$ (notebook, Sec.~20).  Every stage of the
pipeline conserves seniority (the Hamiltonian, the embedding, the path, the
exactly exponentiated pair-hopping blocks and the filter), so the whole
computation can be carried out in the pair basis, one bit per level, which we
validated against the full-sector implementation at $k=4$, $N=4$: the
Hamiltonian and every Trotter term coincide with the seniority-zero blocks to
machine precision, and every anchor of this paper (the energy
$0.6355484736$, $p_0=0.8676375677$, $p(25)=0.9975712826$, the Trotterized
$p=0.9922$ at $T^*=16$, the four-pair ansatz $E=0.6369869810$ and
$1-F=1.776\times10^{-4}$, the exact-ensemble infidelities $1.216\times10^{-3}$,
$6.08\times10^{-4}$, $3.80\times10^{-5}$ and $2.38\times10^{-6}$) is
reproduced.  The gate counts remain those of the $2k$-qubit compilation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:circuits}: a first-order step costs $1\,360$ CNOTs at $k=8$
($248$ rotations, $1\,856$ CNOTs controlled), the coarse term of the $4\to8$
chain is a phase conditioned on the eight appended qubits, $2^8-2=254$ CNOTs
with the Gray-code convention that gave $14$ for the $2\to4$ chain, and the
coarse term of a $6\to8$ chain is the $k=6$ step with every rotation
conditioned on four qubits, $4\,872$ CNOTs.  The one-qubit-per-level pair
encoding, available because the model conserves seniority, is quoted
alongside: there a step costs $112$ CNOTs, a controlled step $240$, a
pair-double rotation $4$ and the $4\to8$ coarse term $14$.

\paragraph{What changes with the size.}
Table~\ref{tab:large} lists the ingredients.  The HF overlap falls from
$0.868$ to $0.690$ at $g=1$ and to $0.25$ at $g=2$, the correlation energy
grows to $1.1$ and $5.5$, and the restricted pair-double ansatz --- now
$16$ occupied-pair$\to$virtual-pair rotations, $768$ CNOTs --- degrades from
the $1.8\times10^{-4}$ of $k=4$ to $3.1\times10^{-3}$ at $g=1$ and
$4.2\times10^{-2}$ at $g=2$: the single-reference variational input is no
longer good.  The response norm of the projector path at its start has the
closed form $\chi_u(0)=P(k-P)\,g^2/(4b^2)$ with $P=N/2$ (verified to
$10^{-9}$ for every $g$; it reduces to $g^2/b^2$ at $k=4$, $N=4$), so at
the shift $\mu_{\rm buf}=0.6$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-refine} it is four
times larger than at $k=4$, and the refinement time grows accordingly:
$T^*=50$ for $1-p<10^{-3}$ instead of $16$.  The shift is the remedy.
Table~\ref{tab:shift} scans $\mu_{\rm buf}$ from $0.6$ to $16$: $T^*$ falls to $8$ at $\mu_{\rm buf}=2$ and $6$ at $\mu_{\rm buf}=4$
for $g=1$, and to $8$ at $\mu_{\rm buf}=8$ for $g=2$, while the number of
second-order steps that reproduce the exact-slice overlap first falls with
the shift and then rises again (the generator norm grows with $\mu$), with a
minimum of $40$ steps for $g=1$ and $80$ for $g=2$.  At the well-chosen
shift all chains $k_{\rm low}\to8$ with $k_{\rm low}=4$ to $7$ collapse onto
one curve (Fig.~\ref{fig:large}, left): the transport is controlled by the
shift-set gap, not by the starting overlap, and a better coarse state buys
nothing once $\mu_{\rm buf}\gtrsim2$.  The HF ramp from the same
determinant, $H(s)=D+\sin^2(\pi s/2)\sum_{pq}h_{pq}$, reaches
$1-p=7\times10^{-5}$ at $T=6$ and its minimum gap is $2\delta$ at every
coupling, so it does not slow down with $g$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:large}, right),
whereas the refinement at fixed shift does ($\chi_u(0)\propto g^2$) and must
be re-tuned.

\paragraph{The digital filter at these norms.}
The Hamiltonian norm is five times larger than at $k=4$, and the
fixed-step filter of Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized} does not survive the
$\delta t=0.25$ operating point: the averaged post-selection map
$\mathcal K$ at $\sigma=4$ (the width that keeps $\sigma\Delta\simeq10$ as
before) has dominant eigenvalue $0.960$ and its eigenoperator has
\emph{zero} overlap with the ground state --- the filter locks onto a
different state of the corrupted evolution, which is the diagnostic
rejection of Fig.~\ref{fig:trotter} in its extreme form.  At
$\delta t=0.1$ the floor is $4.6\times10^{-5}$ with eigenvalue $0.9933$
(acceptance decaying by $0.7\%$ per cycle), at $\delta t=0.05$ it is
$3.1\times10^{-6}$.  We operate at $\delta t=0.1$, where a cycle costs on
average $32$ controlled second-order steps, $1.2\times10^{5}$ CNOTs ---
eleven times the $k=4$ cycle, which is the single most important change: the
filter cycles now dominate every budget.  At $g=2$ the wider spectrum
requires $\sigma=1.5$ ($\sigma\Delta=9$) and $\delta t=0.05$ (floor
$2.5\times10^{-5}$; $3.2\times10^{-4}$ at $0.1$), $9.0\times10^{4}$ CNOTs
per cycle.

\paragraph{Matched comparison.}
Table~\ref{tab:largebudget} and Fig.~\ref{fig:largebudget} repeat the
comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:depth} with ten inputs at $g=1$ and six at $g=2$
(forty common time sets, $M=0$ to $6$, bootstrap standard errors below
$10\%$ for every input with $p>0.99$).  Three things have changed relative
to $k=4$.  First, the unfiltered variational preparation no longer meets
any target: the $16$-pair ansatz needs two, four and six cycles for
$10^{-3}$, $3\times10^{-4}$ and $10^{-4}$, at $2.4\times10^{5}$,
$5.1\times10^{5}$ and $7.4\times10^{5}$ CNOTs per accepted preparation, and
at $g=2$ it needs six cycles for $10^{-3}$ and does not reach
$3\times10^{-4}$.  Refinement followed by the filter is now \emph{cheaper}
than the variational route at every target --- $1.3\times10^{5}$,
$2.4\times10^{5}$ and $5.0\times10^{5}$ CNOTs with the shift
$\mu_{\rm buf}=4$ ($T=6$, $40$ steps), i.e.\ by factors $1.8$, $2.1$ and
$1.5$ --- where at $k=4$ it was $200$--$500$ times more expensive; the
reversal is the combined effect of a poorer ansatz and of costlier cycles.
Second, filtering the bare determinant, which was tied with refinement at
$k=4$, is no longer an option: from $p=0.69$ six cycles reach only
$1.0\times10^{-2}$ ($8.4\times10^{5}$ CNOTs, acceptance $0.67$), and from
$p=0.25$ at $g=2$ only $3\times10^{-2}$.  Third, the ordinary adiabatic
ramp from the same determinant remains the cheapest route at every target
and both couplings --- $1.1\times10^{5}$, $2.2\times10^{5}$ and
$4.8\times10^{5}$ at $g=1$; $2.2\times10^{5}$, $4.0\times10^{5}$ and
$6.0\times10^{5}$ at $g=2$ --- but the margin over refinement, which was a
factor $2.5$--$3$ at $k=4$ with the shift of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-refine},
shrinks to the cost of the embedded coarse term once the shift is tuned:
$18$--$19\%$ at $M=0$, $9$--$10\%$ at the targets that need one or two
cycles and $4$--$7\%$ at those that need three or four, with the same $40$
($g=1$) or $80$ ($g=2$) product-formula steps.  The two adiabatic routes start from the same state and, at a
shift of order the pair-coupling scale $g\sqrt{P(k-P)}/2$, travel at the
same speed; what distinguishes them is only that the projector path
carries a conditioned phase.  A nontrivial coarse stage does not help here:
the $6\to8$ chain from the eight-parameter coarse ansatz starts from
$p_0=0.926$ but its coarse term costs $4\,872$ CNOTs per application, so the
chain costs $10^{6}$ CNOTs before any cycle, and the prolonged $k=6$ state
filtered directly reaches $1.8\times10^{-3}$ after six cycles.  In the pair
encoding every adiabatic cost drops by a factor $12$ and every cycle by $8$,
and the ranking is unchanged (ramp $9.0\times10^{3}$, $2.3\times10^{4}$ and

The answer to the question that motivates this subsection is therefore
split.  A larger and more correlated space does favor refinement followed
by the filter over the variational preparation, and over filtering the bare
determinant, because the single-reference ansatz degrades and the cycles
become expensive while the adiabatic transport, properly shifted, does
not slow down.  It does not favor the shift-controlled projector path over
the ordinary adiabatic ramp from the same determinant, which is cheaper by
the coarse-term overhead at every size and coupling studied; the specific
contribution of resolution refinement --- a coarse eigenstate that is better
than the determinant --- is not exercised by a hierarchy whose coarsest
member is the determinant itself, and a coarse state that is better costs,
in this model, more to prepare and to embed than it saves.  The shift must
be chosen with the size: keeping $\chi_u(0)=P(k-P)g^2/(4b^2)$ of order unity
reproduces the optimum found here, and the value $\mu_{\rm buf}=0.6$ used
at $k=4$ would make the $k=8$ refinement six times slower than necessary.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Discussion and outlook}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ===========================================================================
The pairing model lets us exhibit the complete refinement-plus-filter chain
with every stage benchmarked against exact results: a gate-level UCCSD coarse
solver, a gate-free prolongation with a counted conditioned coarse term, a
gapped adiabatic refinement whose extracted time responds to the
shift-controlled path gap and to the coupling without identifying a scaling
law, and a rodeo filter whose ensemble infidelity follows Eq.~\eqref{eq:fid}
--- all validated a second time at the circuit level with product formulas.
The two algorithms are complementary in the intended sense: refinement
raises the overlap from $0.87$ to $0.998$, and the filter removes the
adiabatic tail geometrically.  The coupling scan adds a practical lesson: at
strong pairing the limiter is not the path gap but the quality of the
prolonged starting state.  The register and the circuits of all stages ---
prolongation, the adiabatic path, the shift rule and the rodeo cycle --- are
written for the generic $2k$-qubit register and do not exploit seniority for
compression; the favorable dynamics and the small variational inputs
available here do rely on the seniority conservation of the pairing force,
and the companion paper~\cite{PHcompanion} examines what changes when a
particle-hole interaction opens the one-particle--one-hole channel, the
price of the more realistic interaction appearing in the Pauli-string
content of the Hamiltonian and hence in the depth of each Trotter step.

The central quantitative finding, however, is negative for the refinement
stage as a gate-saving device.  Compared on the same observable --- the
ground-state infidelity --- and with the same Trotterized filter, refinement
followed by the rodeo algorithm is tied with filtering the unrefined prolonged
determinant, costs four times more than an ordinary adiabatic ramp from the
Hartree--Fock Hamiltonian for the same output overlap, and is beaten by two
orders of magnitude at every target accuracy by the four-parameter
pair-rotation ansatz, which needs no filter at all, at $N=4$ and $N=6$ on
four and six levels (Tables~\ref{tab:depth} and \ref{tab:sizes}).  At four
pairs on eight levels (Sec.~\ref{sec:large}) the variational ansatz is no
longer good, the cycles are eleven times dearer, and refinement plus filter
overtakes both the variational route and the filtered determinant by
factors $1.5$--$2$ and more; but the ordinary ramp from the same
determinant stays ahead by the cost of the conditioned coarse term, at
$g=1$ and at $g=2$, once the shift is scaled with the size.  The reason
is simple, and Sec.~\ref{sec:depth} states it as the ideal large-gap law
$1-\mathcal F_M\simeq\tfrac{1-p}{p}2^{-M}$: a better input saves cycles only
logarithmically in its odds ratio, so moving from $p=0.868$ to $0.998$ saves
some six cycles and raises the asymptotic acceptance from $0.87$ to $1.00$,
and whether those savings repay tens of thousands of refinement gates is a
separate question that, in these systems, they do not --- a single-reference
variational circuit with four parameters already has an overlap above
$0.9998$, and an overlap of $0.87$ costs the filter only a few extra
cycles.  What the refinement offers in exchange is that it is
optimizer-free and measurement-free during preparation, and that its cost is
set by the Hamiltonian evolution rather than by the trainability of a
landscape.  Whether that trade pays off against a variational preparation is, as
Sec.~\ref{sec:large} shows, a question of size and coupling, and the answer
turns in favor of the adiabatic routes already at sixteen qubits; whether
the \emph{shift-controlled} path pays off against the plain ramp is a
question about coarse eigenstates that are better than the determinant,
which a hierarchy seeded by the determinant cannot answer, and about
hardware on which the training measurements of a variational loop
dominate.  We regard the present work as the controlled baseline against
which such claims should be measured, not as evidence for them.

\paragraph{Relation to symmetry-restoration and subspace approaches.}
The results invite a comparison with the pairing-model work of Lacroix and
collaborators summarized in the Introduction.  Both programs start from a
cheap, imperfect trial state and sharpen it; the difference is what is
sharpened and how.  In the symmetry-restoration route the trial state breaks a
symmetry on purpose and the projector --- phase estimation on the symmetry
operator~\cite{Lacroix2020}, an oracle~\cite{RuizGuzman2023} or a
measurement-side projection~\cite{RuizGuzman2024} --- removes the unwanted
sectors, after which a Krylov or phase-estimation stage refines the
energy~\cite{RuizGuzman2022}.  Our coarse state conserves particle number and
spin by construction; what it lacks is \emph{resolution}, and the refinement
supplies it along a gapped path while the rodeo cycle filters in
\emph{energy} rather than in a symmetry quantum number.  The two filters are
structurally the same resource --- one ancilla, a controlled unitary and a
post-selected measurement --- and they compose: a symmetry-projected input
would enter our pipeline exactly as the UCCSD state does.  The quantum
subspace methods of Refs.~\cite{Zhang2025,AychetClaisse2026,BeaujeaultTaudiere2024}
are complementary in a different way: they trade coherent depth for
measurements and a classical generalized eigenvalue problem, and their
accuracy is bounded by the quality of the states that span the subspace,
whereas the rodeo filter converges geometrically in depth but requires the
controlled evolution.  Two observations of that work bear on ours, with the
limits of an energy filter kept in mind.  Ref.~\cite{Zhang2024} documents that
ADAPT-VQE with a symmetry-breaking pool can collapse to unphysical energies on
degenerate pairing systems.  A downstream energy filter can repair such an
input only insofar as the input retains a nonzero overlap with the target:
it cannot recover a state of zero overlap, cannot move weight between
particle-number sectors that the evolution conserves, and cannot separate
exactly degenerate states by energy alone, so a symmetry-broken ADAPT state
would have to be symmetry-projected before, not instead of, the filter.
Ref.~\cite{AychetClaisse2026} shows that the accuracy of the one-body Green's
function of a small superfluid system, computed with the pure pairing
Hamiltonian across the normal-to-superfluid transition driven by the pairing
strength, is controlled by the quality of the even-$N$ ground state; a
filtered state of the kind prepared here is a possible input to that
construction.  The compact pair-to-qubit encoding used
there~\cite{Lacroix2020,AychetClaisse2026} is available precisely because
the pairing force conserves seniority; the particle-hole term of the
companion paper~\cite{PHcompanion}, which is not part of the model of
Ref.~\cite{AychetClaisse2026}, is the ingredient that forces one out of it
and onto the full $2k$-qubit register used throughout this work.

\paragraph{Relation to nucleation.}
The nucleation method of Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} is the closest relative of the
refinement stage, and the comparison is instructive in both directions.  Both
start from a small problem solved exactly on a classical computer and compiled
into a circuit, and both reach the target through a sequence of Trotterized
adiabatic interpolations with the $\sin^2(\pi s/2)$ schedule of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:path}, each of which changes the Hamiltonian only locally:
nucleation switches on the couplings of newly added lattice sites, initialized
in $\ket{+}$, to the existing bulk; refinement switches off the conditioned
coarse term $A$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Aterm} and switches on the matrix elements of
$\Hhigh$ that connect the occupied coarse levels to the appended ones,
initialized empty in $\ket{0}$.  Nucleation grows the domain at fixed
resolution, refinement the resolution at fixed domain, and the two compose in
principle: a lattice or shell-model problem could be nucleated to its physical
size in a coarse basis and then refined to the full basis, or the reverse,
with the same Trotterized machinery and the same filter at the end.

What Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} adds to the present assessment is twofold.  First,
it provides the rigorous sufficient conditions on the total time and on the
Trotter step count of each stage quoted in Sec.~\ref{sec:refine}, and shows on
a twelve-site transverse-field Ising chain that they overestimate the
resources actually needed by orders of magnitude: an overlap of $0.997$ is
reached with $T\simeq40$ and some $2\times10^{3}$ Trotter steps in total,
against bounds of $T\ge2.6\times10^{3}$ and $N\ge2\times10^{10}$.  Section~\ref{sec:res-bound} makes the same comparison for the refinement
path: the time bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Tbound} overshoots the empirical
$T^{*}$ by a factor $3\times10^{2}$ and the step bound overshoots
the steps actually needed by a factor $2\times10^{2}$
(Table~\ref{tab:bound}), because the bound charges the full norm of $B-A$
where the dynamics only pays for the matrix elements that couple the tracked
state to its neighbors; the distance between bound and practice is the room
in which the inverse-gap scaling of Ref.~\cite{Bogner2026} operates.  Second, and more directly
relevant to our negative finding, Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} introduces
\emph{optimized nucleation}: the Trotterized growth circuit is kept as the
ansatz structure and its Trotter angles as the initial parameters, and a
small number $L$ of layers per stage --- far below the Trotter step count of
the non-variational version --- is then optimized layer by layer to minimize
the energy.  On a $5\times5$ transverse-field Ising model this reaches a
lower energy error at fewer two-qubit gates than converting a matrix-product
state to a circuit, even when the latter is itself re-optimized.  The same
construction is available for refinement: the $n_s$ slices of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:path} compiled in Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized} form a
parameterized circuit whose angles are fixed by $(s,\delta t)$, and an
\emph{optimized refinement} with a few layers per stage would trade the one
asset the adiabatic path has over the UCCSD input in Table~\ref{tab:depth}
--- freedom from classical optimization and training measurements --- for a
gate count that could fall well below the $4.96\times10^{4}$ CNOTs of the
$T=30$ path.  Whether a warm-started, physically structured ansatz of this
kind escapes the trainability problems of a generic one is the question
Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} raises for lattices; the pairing model, whose optimum
is known exactly at every resolution, is the place to settle it, and we list
it among the extensions below.

\paragraph{Hardware requirements.}
The Trotterized pipeline of Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized} quantifies the dominant
coherent error of an ideal device, and the requirements it implies are
explicit: $2k+1$ qubits ($9$ at $k=4$, $13$ at $k=6$, $17$ at $k=8$); a
mid-circuit measurement with reset and reuse of one ancilla; arbitrary-angle
$R_z$ rotations, $160$ of them per UCCSD preparation and $60$ per Trotter
step; and a coherent budget that ranges from $192$ two-qubit gates for the
cheapest route of Table~\ref{tab:depth} (the four-pair preparation, no
filter) through $1.7\times10^{4}$ per accepted preparation for the HF ramp
with one cycle to $10^{5}$ for the refined input with six cycles (the companion paper~\cite{PHcompanion} gives the
corresponding budget with the particle-hole term), on top of the energy scan
and the repetitions needed to estimate acceptance probabilities.  At the
two-qubit error rates
demonstrated in large-scale experiments today --- of order $10^{-2}$ on the
superconducting processor of Ref.~\cite{Kim2023} and $(0.8$--$2)\times10^{-3}$
on trapped-ion processors~\cite{Moses2023,Ransford2025} --- a $10^{4}$-gate
run suffers of order ten faults even on the best of these and a
$10^{5}$-gate run of order one hundred; none of these
budgets is executable with physical qubits without substantial error
mitigation, and qubit counts alone say nothing about a device's suitability.
Early logical-qubit demonstrations on neutral-atom arrays have executed
circuits on tens of logical qubits with error detection and, in part, error
correction and mid-circuit readout~\cite{Bluvstein2024,Reichardt2024}, and the
rodeo algorithm has been run in two-qubit form on trapped-ion and
superconducting hardware~\cite{BeeLindgren2022,Qian2024}; a logical-depth
implementation of the budgets above remains to be demonstrated.  A
proportionate near-term target is therefore a mitigated $k=3$ demonstration:
the $2\to3$ chain of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-refine} on seven qubits (six system
qubits plus the ancilla), whose $N=4$ coarse state is a single determinant, or
the $3\to4$ chain of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-k34} on nine qubits, with a short
refinement schedule and one or two rodeo cycles at coarse $\delta t$, i.e.\
$10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ two-qubit gates, where the collapse of the acceptance under
a corrupted evolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:trotterized}) doubles as a hardware
diagnostic.  All-to-all connectivity absorbs the Jordan--Wigner ladders
without SWAP overhead; on square or heavy-hex lattices the weight-4
pair-hopping rotations act on adjacent qubits, which limits but does not
eliminate routing costs.
The full $k=4$ budget is a workload for the first generation of logical-qubit
machines such as the neutral-atom system planned by the QuNorth
initiative~\cite{QuNorth}, and Table~\ref{tab:depth} is offered as its
specification.

\paragraph{Concluding remarks and perspectives.}
The present results establish, on a fully controlled example, how resolution
refinement followed by the rodeo algorithm behaves at the circuit level ---
every stage compiled, counted and validated against exact results --- and
what it is worth: it prepares the correlated ground state to the accuracy of
the Trotterized evolution without any variational optimization, at a gate
cost that, for the pairing model at these sizes, exceeds that of filtering a
cheap variational input.  Three extensions would sharpen the assessment.
First, comparators and compilation: an ADAPT-VQE run proper, a
pair-encoded (one qubit per level) variational and filtering baseline and a
symmetry-projected BCS-like input, an optimized refinement in the sense of
Ref.~\cite{Sadia2026} --- the Trotterized path of Eq.~\eqref{eq:path} as a
warm-started variational circuit with a few layers per stage --- and a
compilation effort applied equally to every route (merged half-steps,
optimized excitation circuits, routing on a concrete connectivity), the
minimum-cost search of Table~\ref{tab:depth} being then repeated over
refinement time, shift and step size with the shot budgets of training and
energy search amortized over the number of states requested.  Second, Hamiltonians for which single-reference
variational inputs fail --- shell-model interactions with competing
deformations, or the strong-coupling regime of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-gscan}
treated with a nontrivial coarse space --- which is where the trainability
argument for refinement would be tested rather than asserted; the companion
paper~\cite{PHcompanion} takes the first step in this direction by adding a
seniority-breaking particle-hole interaction, which degrades the
single-reference ansatz with the strength of the pair breaking.  Third,
hardware: a mitigated seven- or nine-qubit demonstration on present devices,
where the acceptance statistics of the filter double as a diagnostic of the
compiled evolution, followed by the full $k=4$ budget on an early
logical-qubit machine.  The pairing model, exactly solvable at every
resolution, remains the calibration standard for all three.
