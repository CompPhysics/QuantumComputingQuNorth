\section{The sufficient adiabatic time and the response norm of the path}
\label{sm:bound}
% ===========================================================================
Table~\ref{tab:bound} evaluates the sufficient adiabatic time of Jansen,
Ruskai and Seiler~\cite{Jansen2007} in the form used in paper I,
$T_b=\epsilon^{-1/2}\int_0^1[\|\ddot H\|/\Delta^2+7\|\dot H\|^2/\Delta^3]\,ds$
with $\|B-A\|$ the spectral norm of $(\Hhigh-\mu)-P(\Hlow-\mu)P^\dagger$ in the
sector,
once with the sector gap and once with the gap in the symmetry sector of the
tracked state (Fig.~\ref{fig:gaps}), against the empirical $T^*$.  The
symmetry-resolved bound is smaller by a factor $5$ to $17$ but remains three
to five orders of magnitude above $T^*=10/t$, as the pairing bound did
(there $3\times10^2$); the larger ratio here reflects $\|B-A\|=11$--$20$,
set by the shift $\mu$ and the width of the Hubbard spectrum.  For a
rank-one tracked branch the squared projector distance of the bound is the
infidelity, so the two criteria measure the same quantity; the slack comes
from the conservative norm estimates ($\|B-A\|$ and the global gap where
the dynamics pays only for the couplings of the tracked state) and from
comparing $\epsilon=10^{-2}$ with the empirical $10^{-3}$, not from an error
in either.  The table is a numerical quadrature of a rigorous bound, not a
certified enclosure of all discretization errors.

The first-order response norm of the path,
$\chi_u(s)=\sum_{n>0}|\langle n(s)|\partial_uH|0(s)\rangle|^2/[E_n(s)-E_0(s)]^2$
with $u=\sin^2(\pi s/2)$ and $\partial_uH=(\Hhigh-\mu)-P(\Hlow-\mu)P^\dagger$,
is the squared eigenvector response (quantum metric) of the tracked state
along the affine path in $u$; it is a sensitivity diagnostic, not by itself
the leakage, which also depends on the schedule $u'(s)$ (zero at both ends,
so an endpoint maximum of $\chi_u$ is not a maximum of the eigenvector
speed) and on the oscillatory phases set by $T$ times the integrated gaps.
For the pairing model paper I obtained it in closed form at the start of the
path, $\chi_u(0)=\|Q\Hhigh P\psi_{\rm low}\|^2/\mu_{\rm buf}^2$.
Figure~\ref{fig:ramp} (left) follows it along Path~A for the chain $4\to8$:
$\chi_u(0)=0.375$, $0.527$, $0.715$ and $0.874$ for $U/t=0,2,4,8$, the
maximum sits at the initial endpoint for every coupling, and the norm falls
by a factor two to four along the path (at $U=8t$ it rises again toward
$s=1$, to $0.45$, as $\Deff$ shrinks).  The shift fixes the initial value as
it did for the pairing model, and the growth of $\chi_u(0)$ with $U$ is
modest, consistent with (not an explanation of) the $T^*$ that does not
change with $U$ over the tested range.  The right panel compares, with exact
slices, the ordinary adiabatic ramp from the free-fermion determinant with
the two refinement paths at $U=4t$: the ramp reaches $p=0.96878$,
$0.99686$, $0.99960$ and $0.99989$ at $T=4,8,12,16$ (and $0.99999$ at
$24/t$).

\section{The dimer-seeded hierarchy and the composition of steps}
\label{sm:dimer}

coarse chain the 26-parameter ansatz is exact at $U=0$ and reaches
infidelities $1.8\times10^{-4}$, $3.3\times10^{-3}$ and $1.7\times10^{-2}$

\section{Trotterized refinement at eight sites}
\label{sm:trotter}
% ===========================================================================
Figure~\ref{fig:trotter} replaces the exact slice propagation at $T=16/t$,
$U=4t$ by first- and second-order product formulas over the groups
$[P(\Hlow-\mu)P^\dagger,\,K_{\rm even},\,K_{\rm odd},\,D-\mu]$ for Path~A
and $[(1+\Lambda\cos^2)K_{\rm even},\,K_{\rm odd},\,D]$ for Path~B.  The
second-order formula reproduces the exact-slice overlap to within $10^{-3}$
with $n_s=128$ steps for Path~A ($p=0.99983$), $256$ for Path~B with
$\Lambda=8t$ ($0.99213$) and $512$ for $\Lambda=16t$ ($0.99342$);
first-order formulas do not reach it within $10^{-3}$ even at $512$ steps.
The counted circuit of Path~A does not exponentiate the embedded coarse
term as one block: on a device it is the coarse Hubbard step, i.e.\ the
three conditioned coarse groups $P(D_c-\mu)P^\dagger$,
$PK^c_{\rm even}P^\dagger$ and $PK^c_{\rm odd}P^\dagger$, which do not
commute.  Repeating the second-order refinement with the coarse term split
accordingly (six groups in all) gives $p=0.997299$, $0.999818$, $0.999941$
and $0.999943$ at $n_s=64$, $128$, $256$ and $512$, against $0.997456$,
$0.999831$, $0.999941$ and $0.999943$ with the coarse term as one block;
the step count $n_s=128$ and the cost of Path~A are unchanged, and the
split-group state is the one used in Table~\ref{tab:budget}.  The ramp's
product-formula version reproduces its exact-slice overlap with $64$
second-order steps at $T=8/t$ ($0.996891$ against $0.996861$) and gives
$0.999659$ (exact $0.999885$) with $128$ steps at $T=16/t$.

The embedded coarse term of Path~A at eight sites is the coarse Hubbard
step on the bonding register --- $24$ rotations, $32$ CNOTs unconditioned
--- with every rotation conditioned on the eight antibonding qubits being
empty, plus the conditioned scalar phase.  With the Gray-code
phase-polynomial convention of paper I (exact for few controls but growing
as $2^{c+1}$) this is $1.3\times10^4$ CNOTs per application; with a
clean-ancilla ladder of relative-phase Toffolis per rotation $1\,132$; with
the conjunction of the controls computed once into a shared flag, every
rotation singly controlled by it and the flag uncomputed, $124$, plus the
frame change into and out of the bonding basis ($32$), i.e.\ $156$ CNOTs
per application, the figure used throughout (Sec.~\ref{sec:circuits}); the
shared flag needs $c-1=7$ clean work qubits.  In the block Jordan--Wigner ordering the $Z_jZ_{j+L_f}$ terms
are the only non-local ones; the site-major ordering, which makes them
adjacent at the price of weight-three hopping strings, is the alternative
to be compared on a linear-connectivity device.

\section{Ingredients of the thirty-two-qubit study}
\label{sm:large}

\paragraph{Product formulas through the Kronecker structure.}
In the $(2,2)$ sector of the sixteen-site chain ($14\,400$ states) the dense
machinery of the eight-site study is unavailable: a $14\,400^2$ complex step
unitary cannot be stored, let alone diagonalized.  Every term of the
product formula is instead applied through Eq.~\eqref{eq:kron}.  A hopping
layer $K_{\rm layer}=K_\uparrow\otimes\mathbb1+\mathbb1\otimes K_\downarrow$
acts on $\psi$ reshaped as a $120\times120$ matrix as
$e^{-icK_\uparrow}\,\Psi\,e^{-icK_\downarrow^T}$, with the two $120\times120$
exponentials obtained from one diagonalization per species; the diagonal
interaction is an elementwise phase; and the embedded coarse term of Path~A,
$P\,X\,P^\dagger$ with $X$ one of the three $784\times784$ conditioned coarse
groups, is applied exactly as
$\psi\mapsto\psi+P\big[V(e^{-ic\lambda}-1)V^\dagger\big]P^\dagger\psi$, with
$X=V\lambda V^\dagger$ diagonalized once.  The second-order step is the
symmetric product of these factors.  The ramp uses the groups
$[K_{\rm even},K_{\rm odd},D]$ with the interaction coefficient
$\sin^2(\pi s/2)$, Path~B the same groups with the even (intra-block) layer
scaled by $1+\Lambda\cos^2(\pi s/2)$, and Path~A the six groups
$[P(D_c-\mu)P^\dagger,PK^c_{\rm even}P^\dagger,PK^c_{\rm odd}P^\dagger,
K_{\rm even},K_{\rm odd},D-\mu]$ with the $\cos^2$/$\sin^2$ coefficients of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pathA}.  The exact-slice transports use $48$ slices with a
sparse Krylov exponential.  Applied to the eight-site chain, where the
dense machinery exists, the Kronecker implementation reproduces the ramp,
the split-group Path-A refinement and the filter tracks of
Sec.~\ref{sec:budget} to $10^{-13}$.

\paragraph{The Trotter-effective ground state.}
The fixed-step filter projects, in the long run, on the eigenvector of the
step unitary $U_{\delta t}$ closest to the ground state, and its eigenphase
is the objective energy $\Eobj$.  At eight sites that eigenvector was read
off a Schur decomposition of the step; at sixteen sites it is obtained by
iterating the quantized-time filter itself: starting from the exact ground
state, $\psi\mapsto\tfrac12(\psi+e^{i\Eobj n\delta t}U_{\delta t}^n\psi)$
with $n$ drawn from the Gaussian ensemble of cycle times and $\Eobj$
updated at every iteration to the eigenphase estimate
$-\arg\expval{U_{\delta t}}{\psi}/\delta t$, converges in a few tens of iterations to the fixed point of the
filter, which is that eigenvector.  At eight sites the procedure reproduces
the Schur result ($\Eobj=-5.620907$, floor $5.8\times10^{-5}$) to the
quoted digits; at sixteen sites, with $\sigma=36.2/t$ and $\delta t=0.1/t$,
it gives $\Eobj-E_0=+4.92\times10^{-3}t$ and a floor of
$3.7\times10^{-5}$, close to the eight-site floor at the same step, as
expected for a local error per step that is the same at both sizes.

\paragraph{Gate counts at sixteen sites.}
The second-order step of the sixteen-site chain costs $304$ CNOTs ($608$
controlled, the scalar single-$Z$ sum absorbed into the ancilla phase), the
Givens network of the free-fermion determinant $2\times120$ rotations,
$480$ CNOTs, the prolongation $8\to16$ $32$ CNOTs, and the coarse term of
Path~A --- the eight-site Hubbard step, $52$ rotations, conditioned on the
sixteen antibonding qubits through one shared flag, plus the conditioned
scalar phase and the frame change --- $268+64=332$ CNOTs per application
($4\,948$ with a relative-phase Toffoli ladder per rotation, the convention
of an earlier version), applied twice per second-order step.  The coarse
state of the hierarchy is not an oracle: it is the state actually prepared
by the cheapest eight-site unfiltered route of Sec.~\ref{sec:budget}
below $3\times10^{-4}$ (the ramp $T=16/t$, $n_s=192$: $1.8\times10^{-4}$,
$27\,760$ CNOTs) or below $10^{-4}$ ($T=24/t$, $n_s=256$:
$5.6\times10^{-5}$, $36\,976$ CNOTs), prolonged ($p_0=0.8689$ and
$0.8688$ against $0.8692$) and transported, and its cost is charged to
every input that starts from it; the search of Table~\ref{tab:large}
picks between the two, and the cheaper one wins at every target.

\paragraph{Step scans.}
With the step scans of the notebook (second order, $n_s=32$ to $512$,
accepted when the overlap agrees with the exact-slice value to $10^{-3}$):
the ramp needs $n_s=128$ at $T=16/t$ ($0.96927$ against $0.96970$;
$0.91802$ at $n_s=64$) and $256$ at $T=32/t$ ($0.99556$ against $0.99591$;
$0.00545$, $0.94463$ and $0.99304$ at $n_s=32$, $64$, $128$ --- a step of
$\delta t=1/t$ destroys the transport); Path~B with $\Lambda=8t$ needs
$256$ at $T=16/t$ ($0.99476$ against $0.99492$; $4\times10^{-5}$, $0.266$
and $0.98688$ at $n_s=32$, $64$, $128$, the intra-block generator of norm
$1+\Lambda$ requiring $\delta t\lesssim0.06/t$); Path~A with
$\mu_{\rm buf}=2t$ and the coarse groups split needs $64$ steps at $T=8/t$
($0.99228$ against $0.99237$; $0.99047$ at $n_s=32$) and $128$ at $T=16/t$
($0.99930$ against $0.99931$; $0.94602$ and $0.99744$ at $n_s=32$, $64$).
An earlier version of this study reported a plateau of Path~A at $T=8/t$,
$1.6\times10^{-3}$ below the exact value at every step count, and explained
it by the splitting of non-commuting coarse groups; the explanation was
wrong.  The embedded path had been built with the Galerkin constant $-tN_c$
instead of $-tN$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gal1d}), i.e.\ with an extra term
$-4t\cos^2(\pi s/2)PP^\dagger$ that is not a global phase, and with the
correct constant the product formula converges to the exact-slice result at
every $T$, as it must for a consistent decomposition.

\paragraph{Sector gap along the ramp.}
The sector gap of $H_R(s)$ falls monotonically from $0.165t$ at $s=0$ to
$0.051t$ at $s=1$ ($0.125t$, $0.078t$, $0.057t$ at $s=0.25,0.5,0.75$); the
ramp from the determinant is governed, like Path~A, by the gap in the
singlet, reflection-even sector, which is why both transports converge at
all with a sector gap of $0.05t$.

Path~A over six $(T,n_s)$ pairs from $(8,64)$ to $(32,512)$.  L1 is a fine
grid of the evolution time --- at eight sites $T=6$--$48/t$ (ramp) and
$6$--$24/t$ (Path~A) in steps of $1/t$ and $12$--$32/t$ (Path~B) in steps
of $2/t$; at sixteen sites $16$--$80/t$ in steps of $4/t$ (ramp) and
$4$--$40/t$ in steps of $2/t$ (Path~A) --- with the second-order step
count on a geometric list of ratio $1.15$ ($32$--$768$, $64$--$1\,024$ for
Path~B, $64$--$768$ and $24$--$512$ at sixteen sites) and, for Path~A and
B, both charged coarse states and $\Lambda\in\{8,16,32\}t$; at eight sites
every point is evaluated with the exact quantized-time ensemble for
$M=0,\ldots,6$, at sixteen sites unfiltered ($M=0$), which is complete for
the ramp and Path~A because the expected cost of any filtered point is
at least its input plus one average cycle, $1.76\times10^5$ CNOTs, above
their unfiltered minima at every target of Table~\ref{tab:large}.  L2
refines, for every control whose L1 cost lies within $40\%$ of the L1
minimum, the integer step count by bracketing and bisection at fixed
$(T,M)$ and control.  The coarse state of the $8\to16$ hierarchy is
re-selected at L1 as the cheapest refined eight-site unfiltered ramp
below $3\times10^{-4}$ (c: $T=13/t$, $n_s=113$, $1-F=3.0\times10^{-4}$,
$16\,416$ CNOTs with the prolongation, against $27\,792$ in the first
search) and below $10^{-4}$ (a: $T=19/t$, $n_s=167$, $9.9\times10^{-5}$,
$24\,192$ against $37\,008$).  Table~\ref{tab:converge} lists the three
levels, the L2 operating point, the number of distinct controls ($T$, or
$(\Lambda,T)$ with the coarse state) whose refined cost lies within $10\%$
of the L2 minimum, and the ratio Path~A/ramp.  Figures~\ref{fig:grid8} and
\ref{fig:grid16} show the L0 grids themselves with the exact-ensemble or
sampled tracks of the fixed inputs; the frontier curves of
Fig.~\ref{fig:frontiers} of the main text are read from the L1 clouds.

Three features of Table~\ref{tab:converge} carry the conclusions drawn
in the main text.  First, the first grids were not converged: at eight
sites every adiabatic minimum falls on refinement, by $28$--$44\%$ for the
ramp, $20$--$22\%$ for Path~A and $15$--$23\%$ for Path~B, and at sixteen
sites by $25$--$63\%$ for the ramp and $18$--$52\%$ for Path~A, the largest
changes at the loose targets, where the first grid's shortest time and
smallest step count were already too long and too many.  Second, the fine
grids are close to converged in the sense available to a finite search:
the integer refinement L1$\to$L2 moves the minima by $0$--$11\%$, and the
unfiltered frontiers are sharp in $T$ (one to six controls within $10\%$
of the minimum) because the cheapest operating point sits at the shortest
time at which the adiabatic error alone meets the target, after which the
step count is fixed by the digitization error; the filtered Path-B
frontier at eight sites is flatter ($5$--$14$ controls within $10\%$), as
cycles and steps trade against each other there.  We do not interpret
differences between routes below the $10\%$ level.  Third, the ratio of
the projector path to the ramp is $2.95$--$3.53$ at eight sites and
$1.60$--$1.86$ at sixteen, uniformly in the ramp's favor: the first grid's
near-tie at thirty-two qubits ($1.5$ against $1.6\times10^5$ CNOTs at
$10^{-3}$) was an artifact of its $T$ spacing --- the ramp's minimum
moved from $(48/t,512)$ to $(44/t,244)$ and halved --- and the
fifteen-percent margin at $10^{-2}$ likewise ($(32/t,256)$ to $(28/t,94)$).
The cheaper coarse states selected by the refined eight-site search
($16\,416$ and $24\,192$ CNOTs instead of $27\,792$ and $37\,008$) lower the
Path-A costs by $1$--$2\times10^4$ CNOTs and are included in the L1 and L2
values; the Path-B and prolonged-state rows of Table~\ref{tab:large},
which keep the first search's coarse states, would gain the same amount
and remain far from the frontier.
